# Compliance Relief for Well-Established Technologies: EC Delegated Acts Expand MDR Clinical Investigation Exemptions

## 1. Policy Background & Significance

Since its adoption in 2017 and full application in 2021, Regulation (EU) 2017/745 (the "MDR") has imposed substantially stricter clinical evaluation and clinical investigation requirements than its predecessors—the Active Implantable Medical Devices Directive (AIMDD, 90/385/EEC) and the Medical Devices Directive (MDD, 93/42/EEC). Under Article 61(4) of the MDR, implantable devices and Class III devices must, in principle, base their clinical evaluation on clinical investigation data, unless the device falls within the exemptions set out in Article 61(6).

The "Well-Established Technologies" (WET) exemption under Article 61(6)(b) is a critical mechanism for reducing the compliance burden on mature devices. This provision recognises that for device types with relatively simple designs, well-known safety and clinical performance characteristics, and a long history on the market, conducting clinical investigations is objectively "inappropriate"—and empowers the European Commission to list such devices by means of delegated acts. Similarly, Article 54(2) of the MDR exempts certain Class IIb implantable devices from the per-device Clinical Evaluation Consultation Procedure (CECP), with the list also amendable through delegated acts.

In the early years of MDR implementation, however, both lists were relatively narrow in scope, covering mainly sutures, staples, dental fillings, dental braces, tooth crowns, screws, wedges, plates, wires, pins, clips, and connectors. A significant number of Class IIb implantable devices and Class III devices with mature designs and well-established clinical indications were still required to conduct clinical investigations or undergo per-device technical documentation reviews, leading to markedly longer certification timelines, sharply higher compliance costs, and—indirectly—a "device availability crisis" in the EU market, with some traditional devices being withdrawn or restricted in supply.

Against this backdrop, "regulatory simplification" has become a priority on the European Commission's health policy agenda. In 2025, the Commission established a dedicated Task Force under the Medical Device Coordination Group (MDCG), which developed the expanded WET list following a public call for evidence that enabled stakeholder input. On 20 March 2026, the Commission formally signed two delegated acts—**Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2026/1451** and **Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2026/1359**. Both were published in the Official Journal of the EU on **29 June 2026** and will enter into force 20 days later, on **19 July 2026**.

These delegated acts represent a concrete implementation of the EU's principle of differentiated regulation between well-established technologies and high-risk innovative devices—offering tangible and actionable compliance relief for manufacturers of sutures, staplers, bone screws, and other traditional surgical devices.

## 2. Core Changes Analysis

### 2.1 Delegated Regulation (EU) 2026/1451: Expansion of the Exemption List for Implantable and Class III Devices

This regulation amends the list of implantable devices and Class III devices exempted from the obligation to perform clinical investigations under Article 61(6)(b) of the MDR, adding more device types that meet the definition of well-established technologies. The underlying rationale is that for devices with stable designs and safety profiles demonstrated over long periods of clinical use, mandating new clinical investigations offers limited scientific value, may raise ethical feasibility concerns, and imposes a disproportionate regulatory burden.

With this expansion, implantable and Class III devices listed in (EU) 2026/1451 may be exempted from the clinical investigation requirement of Article 61(4) when the following conditions are met:

1. The device type falls within the WET list set out in (EU) 2026/1451;
2. The manufacturer is able to complete a sufficient clinical evaluation in accordance with Article 61(3), demonstrating conformity with the general safety and performance requirements (GSPR) in Annex I;
3. The clinical evaluation is based on adequate clinical data sources, including but not limited to published scientific literature, clinical data from equivalent devices, post-market surveillance (PMS) data, and post-market clinical follow-up (PMCF) data.

It is essential to emphasise that **the exemption applies to the obligation to "conduct clinical investigations," not to the obligation to "perform clinical evaluation."** Manufacturers must still prepare and maintain a Clinical Evaluation Report (CER) in accordance with Article 61 and Annex XIV, and must continuously collect PMCF data throughout the device lifecycle.

### 2.2 Delegated Regulation (EU) 2026/1359: Expansion of the Exemption List for Class IIb Implantables from Per-Device Technical Documentation Assessment

Under the MDR framework, for Class IIb implantable devices not on the exemption list, the Notified Body (NB) must apply the Clinical Evaluation Consultation Procedure under Article 54 for every device—i.e., submitting the manufacturer's clinical evaluation summary to an independent expert panel for consultation. While this "per-device review" mechanism has value in terms of risk control, its application to Class IIb implantables of well-established technology is manifestly disproportionate to the actual risk profile of such devices, significantly increasing NB workload and manufacturer certification lead times.

Delegated Regulation (EU) 2026/1359 amends Article 54(2) of the MDR to further expand the exemption list for Class IIb implantable devices. For newly exempted device types, Notified Bodies will no longer need to consult the expert panel for each device and may complete conformity assessment through the routine procedures under Article 52. This will materially shorten NB review timelines for Class IIb implantables with mature designs and well-characterised clinical performance.

### 2.3 Definition Criteria for Well-Established Technologies and the Logic of List Expansion

Both delegated acts are founded on the following core characteristics of well-established technology devices:

- **Relatively simple design**: common, stable device architecture with slow technological evolution;
- **Well-known safety and clinical performance characteristics**: supported by extensive, long-term published clinical data and real-world evidence;
- **Long history of market use**: a track record of safe use in the EU and major global markets over many years.

The MDCG Task Force, during the 2025 call for evidence, established a key principle: a device type should only be included on the exemption list where clinical investigations are **scientifically "clearly inappropriate" or ethically infeasible** for that device type. This principle ensures that regulatory simplification does not compromise the fundamental baseline of patient safety.

## 3. Impact Analysis

### 3.1 Impact on Manufacturers

For manufacturers of affected product lines, this update has a direct and substantial positive impact. First, manufacturers of implantable and Class III devices listed under (EU) 2026/1451 will no longer need to initiate new clinical investigations for MDR initial certification or major changes—potentially saving projects that typically take 2–5 years and cost hundreds of thousands to millions of euros. Second, manufacturers of Class IIb implantables listed under (EU) 2026/1359 can avoid per-device CECP reviews, shortening NB assessment timelines and reducing associated delays and costs.

However, the exemption is not a "free pass." Manufacturers must still complete:

- Clinical evaluation and a CER compliant with Article 61 and Annex XIV;
- The technical documentation required for NB conformity assessment;
- A quality management system compliant with Annex IX or XI;
- Robust PMS and PMCF plans and data.

Whether a manufacturer can effectively invoke the exemption ultimately depends on the strength of the justification in the technical documentation—specifically, the demonstration of the device's WET attributes and the sufficiency of clinical data to support GSPR conformity.

### 3.2 Impact on the Industry

This update sends a clear regulatory policy signal: the European Commission is moving from the "strict uniform enforcement" of the early MDR implementation period towards "risk-based, proportionate regulation." For mature products with long service records in the European market, the regulatory system is now recognising that accumulated clinical experience and real-world data can adequately support clinical safety demonstrations—without requiring repeated prospective studies for every baseline design iteration.

This shift helps alleviate the compliance-driven exit of certain traditional devices from the EU market since MDR implementation, improving device availability. Simultaneously, Notified Bodies benefit from reduced CECP review volumes, freeing up assessment resources that can be redirected toward in-depth reviews of genuinely high-risk and innovative devices—improving overall regulatory efficiency.

### 3.3 Impact on Patients and Users

Reduced compliance costs translate directly into improved availability and continuity of supply of mature devices in the EU market. In areas such as sutures, staplers, and orthopaedic bone screws, surgeons and patients can continue to access traditional devices validated over decades of clinical use—avoiding product shortages or specification reductions caused by disproportionate compliance burdens.

It must be clarified that the exemption provisions are still premised on adequate clinical evaluation and ongoing PMCF data, and do not represent a substantive concession on patient safety standards. On the contrary, redirecting clinical investigation resources away from "repeated validation of mature devices" toward the assessment and oversight of high-risk and innovative products helps improve the overall allocation efficiency of regulatory resources and the level of patient protection.

## 4. Compliance Recommendations

### 4.1 Immediately Conduct a Portfolio Eligibility Screening

Manufacturers should, immediately upon entry into force of the regulations (19 July 2026), review the full text of the two delegated acts' lists against their existing product portfolios, verifying item-by-item which products fall within the expanded WET lists. It is recommended to establish a mapping table of "product—classification—applicable exemption provision—current clinical evaluation status" and complete an initial internal screening within 30 days of entry into force to identify which products can immediately benefit from the new exemptions.

### 4.2 Update Clinical Evaluation Plans and Clinical Evaluation Reports

For products newly included in the exemption lists, manufacturers should systematically update their Clinical Evaluation Plans (CEPs) and Clinical Evaluation Reports (CERs), explicitly citing the applicable exemption provisions and clearly justifying that the device meets the WET definition criteria. CERs should include the following key elements:

- Evidence of design maturity and market history;
- Literature review and comparative analysis of equivalent devices;
- PMS data summary and PMCF plan;
- A clear conclusion: sufficient clinical evidence supports GSPR conformity without the need for new clinical investigations.

### 4.3 Proactively Engage with Notified Bodies

For products already in the MDR certification process whose technical documentation review has not yet been completed, manufacturers should proactively contact their Notified Body to confirm whether the assessment pathway can be adjusted based on the expanded exemption lists. For Class IIb implantables, if a product now falls within the (EU) 2026/1359 exemption list, manufacturers may request the NB to terminate or adjust an already-commenced CECP procedure, depending on the specific certification stage.

### 4.4 Update Technical Documentation and QMS Documents

It is recommended to add a "WET Exemption Compliance Declaration" to the technical documentation, explicitly citing the relevant delegated regulation article numbers and annexing an index of supporting evidence. In parallel, update the quality management system's standard operating procedures (SOPs) related to clinical evaluation to incorporate the conditions for invoking WET exemptions, justification requirements, and internal approval workflows into controlled documents—ensuring consistency and traceability when exemptions are invoked.

### 4.5 Monitor Future Expansions and Interpretive Documents

The European Commission may further expand the WET lists through new delegated acts in the future. In addition, MDCG is expected to publish accompanying guidance documents providing more detailed interpretation of the justification requirements for WET exemptions. It is recommended to designate responsible personnel to continuously monitor MDCG activities and regulatory publications in the Official Journal, ensuring that the compliance strategy remains aligned with regulatory evolution.

It is important to note that the two delegated regulations **apply directly from their date of entry into force, without any transition period**. This means manufacturers may invoke the new exemption provisions in MDR certification activities from 19 July 2026 onwards, without waiting for any transposition measures at Member State level. For products already certified under the MDR that fall within the expanded WET lists, the exemptions apply automatically upon entry into force; however, manufacturers are advised to supplement their compliance declarations during the next periodic technical documentation update to avoid ambiguity during NB surveillance audits.

In conclusion, the publication of these two delegated acts marks a pragmatic shift in EU MDR regulation—from the initial "undifferentiated strictness" towards "risk-based, proportionate regulation." For manufacturers of well-established technology medical devices, seizing this policy window by systematically updating clinical evaluation and technical documentation strategies will effectively reduce compliance costs, shorten certification timelines, and provide institutional assurance for the long-term, stable supply of products in the European market.
