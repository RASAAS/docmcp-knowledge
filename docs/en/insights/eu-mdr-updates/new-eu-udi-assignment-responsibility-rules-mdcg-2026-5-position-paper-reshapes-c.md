## 1. Policy Background & Significance

The Unique Device Identifier (UDI) system is a core traceability tool introduced by the EU Medical Devices Regulation (Regulation (EU) 2017/745, MDR) and the In Vitro Diagnostic Medical Devices Regulation (Regulation (EU) 2017/746, IVDR). Under Article 27 MDR and Article 24 IVDR, manufacturers must assign a UDI to a device before placing it on the market and ensure the UDI carrier is affixed to the device label or packaging. Manufacturers must also assign a Basic UDI-DI to the device, which links to certificates, declarations of conformity, and technical documentation. Annex VI Part C of the MDR further specifies the circumstances under which a new UDI-DI must be assigned.

However, the involvement of non-manufacturer entities in the supply chain—particularly distributors—has long created legal uncertainty regarding UDI assignment responsibilities. Article 16 of both the MDR and the IVDR provides that a distributor or importer who places a device on the market under its own name or trademark, changes the intended purpose of a device, or modifies a device in a way that may affect compliance with applicable requirements, must assume the obligations incumbent on manufacturers. Although this mechanism is clear in principle, divergent interpretations have persisted among industry and Member State enforcement authorities regarding who must assign a new UDI-DI, a new Basic UDI-DI, and who is responsible for submitting the relevant data to EUDAMED in specific scenarios such as relabelling, repackaging, and private labelling.

On 22 July 2026, the Medical Device Coordination Group (MDCG) published Position Paper MDCG 2026-5, “UDI assignment between manufacturers and distributors.” Listed under the UDI section of the official MDCG guidance page, this document systematically clarifies the chain of UDI assignment responsibility among manufacturers, importers, and distributors. It follows earlier guidance such as MDCG 2018-6 (clarifications of UDI related responsibilities in relation to Article 16), MDCG 2021-26 (Q&A on repackaging and relabelling activities under Article 16), and MDCG 2021-23 (guidance on certification activities under Article 16(4)). While MDCG position papers are not legally binding, they represent a common understanding aimed at effective and harmonised implementation of the legislation, and are therefore highly significant for uniform enforcement and compliance risk reduction.

The core value of this position paper lies in its realignment of UDI assignment rules dispersed across MDR/IVDR provisions, annexes, and existing MDCG guidance, establishing clear responsibility boundaries among manufacturers, importers, and distributors—especially for the high-risk, high-controversy scenarios of private labelling and parallel trade. For all supply chain participants dealing with medical devices and IVDs on the EU market, timely understanding and implementation of this document is an essential prerequisite for complying with MDR/IVDR traceability obligations.

## 2. Core Changes Analysis

### 2.1 Relabelling / Repackaging Triggering Manufacturer Obligations and UDI Reassignment

MDCG 2026-5 first reiterates and clarifies the logic of Article 16(1)(a)–(c) MDR. Under that provision, any distributor, importer, or other natural or legal person who:

- makes available on the market a device under its own name, registered trade name, or registered trade mark (Article 16(1)(a));
- changes the intended purpose of a device already placed on the market or put into service (Article 16(1)(b)); or
- modifies a device already placed on the market or put into service in such a way that compliance with the applicable requirements may be affected (Article 16(1)(c)),

shall assume the obligations incumbent on manufacturers.

The position paper explicitly states: when a distributor performs **relabelling** that changes device identification information (e.g., device name, trademark, legal manufacturer information, intended purpose, or any label element affecting device identification and safe use), that distributor becomes a “manufacturer” under Article 16(1)(a) or (c) and **must cease using the original manufacturer’s UDI-DI and assign a new UDI-DI to the relabelled device**. This requirement likewise applies to **repackaging**—if repackaging results in the device label being replaced, covered, or rendered unreadable, manufacturer obligations are triggered. However, if repackaging is purely for logistics purposes and does not alter the device label, with the original label remaining clearly legible and the UDI carrier unobstructed, the original UDI-DI may be retained, with no reassignment required.

This resolves a common practical confusion: many distributors add their own logistics labels to outer shipping boxes without realising that the UDI-DI on the inner device label remains that of the original manufacturer; conversely, some distributors relabel without assigning a new UDI-DI, resulting in a single device having two legal manufacturer identities on the market and contradictory UDI-DI data in EUDAMED. MDCG 2026-5 makes clear that once Article 16 is triggered, the relabelling/repackaging party becomes the “manufacturer” for that version of the device, and its UDI assignment obligation is independent of the original manufacturer; it cannot refuse to act on the grounds that “the device already has a UDI.”

### 2.2 UDI and Basic UDI-DI Assignment in Private Label Scenarios

Private labelling refers to a distributor placing on the market a device manufactured by an original manufacturer under the distributor’s own name or trademark, with the original manufacturer not appearing on the label or registration information. Under Article 16(1)(a) MDR, a private label distributor is deemed a manufacturer unless it has an agreement with the original manufacturer whereby the original manufacturer is identified as such on the label and is responsible for meeting manufacturer requirements. That exception is rarely met in practice.

MDCG 2026-5 takes a strict position on private labelling: **a private label distributor must assign a new Basic UDI-DI for each device version/model placed on the market under its own name or trademark, and assign the corresponding UDI-DI accordingly.** The original manufacturer’s Basic UDI-DI may not be reused by the private label distributor, as this would break the device identity chain across notified body certificates, declarations of conformity, post-market surveillance reports, and other documents. At the same time, the private label distributor must comply with all general manufacturer obligations under Article 10 MDR, including establishing a quality management system, preparing technical documentation, implementing post-market surveillance, and reporting serious incidents and field safety corrective actions. Although Article 16(4) allows such a “new manufacturer” to rely on the original manufacturer’s conformity assessment under certain conditions, this reliance does not exempt it from UDI assignment and EUDAMED registration obligations.

MDCG 2026-5 also reminds private label distributors to observe UDI-DI change rules when assigning new UDI-DIs. If a subsequent substantive change occurs (e.g., performance, intended purpose, sterilisation method), a new UDI-DI must be assigned in accordance with Annex VI Part C MDR and MDCG 2018-1 rev.4, while the old UDI-DI is retained for historical traceability.

### 2.3 Boundaries for Parallel Trade and Inter-Member State Language Label Adjustments

Parallel trade refers to a device lawfully placed on the market in one Member State being imported by a distributor into another Member State for sale, often requiring the addition of translated labels or over-stickers to meet the language requirements of the destination Member State. Whether such activities trigger Article 16 manufacturer obligations has been a frequent controversy in UDI compliance.

MDCG 2026-5 provides a relatively detailed distinction:

- **Where only a translation label is added that does not obscure the original label (e.g., an information leaflet or sticker in the official language of the destination Member State placed on the outer packaging), and the UDI carrier, device name, model, batch/serial number, expiry date, and other information on the original label remain unchanged**, this is not considered relabelling, and the distributor does not need to assign a new UDI-DI. The original manufacturer’s UDI-DI remains valid.
- **If the translation label covers or partially covers the UDI carrier, device identification information, or safety warnings on the original label, or if the original label must be removed before affixing the translation label**, this constitutes relabelling, and the distributor must assume manufacturer obligations and assign a new UDI-DI.
- **If the distributor changes any identification element of the device in the course of parallel trade (e.g., using its own trade name, changing the device name or intended purpose description), it falls directly under Article 16(1)(a) or (b) and must assign a new UDI-DI and comply with all manufacturer obligations.**

The significance of this distinction is that it provides a clear “safe harbour” for parallel traders: language supplementation that keeps the original label intact and readable does not trigger manufacturer obligations; once the original label content is touched, the UDI reassignment process must be initiated.

### 2.4 Responsibility for EUDAMED Data Submission and UDI-DI Change Management

The position paper emphasises that **UDI assignment responsibility is inseparable from EUDAMED data submission responsibility.** Under Articles 29 and 31 MDR, manufacturers are obliged to submit UDI-DI and related device data in the EUDAMED device registration module. When a distributor or importer becomes a “manufacturer” under Article 16, it must register itself in EUDAMED as an actor (manufacturer) and submit its assigned UDI-DI and device data in that capacity; it may not require the original manufacturer to submit on its behalf. The original manufacturer only needs to maintain registration data corresponding to its original UDI-DI.

MDCG 2026-5 specifically notes that the same physical device may have multiple UDI-DIs in the supply chain due to successive relabelling/repackaging. To ensure a complete traceability chain, each responsible party should establish a mapping relationship “original UDI-DI—new UDI-DI” in internal records and, where appropriate, document this via EUDAMED linking functions or in technical documentation. All UDI-DI changes must comply with Annex VI Part C MDR and MDCG 2018-1 rev.4 change rules, ensuring that a new UDI-DI is used only for a device version where identification attributes have genuinely changed.

### 2.5 Interface with MDCG 2018-6 and MDCG 2021-26

MDCG 2026-5 does not exist in isolation but consolidates and upgrades existing guidance. MDCG 2018-6 first clarified that importers/distributors assuming manufacturer obligations under Article 16 must assign a UDI; MDCG 2021-26 answered further operational questions such as “what kind of repackaging constitutes relabelling” and “whether translation labels trigger obligations.” Building on these, MDCG 2026-5, against the new regulatory environment of gradually mandatory EUDAMED use, provides clearer positions on private labelling and parallel trade, and emphasises the independent assignment requirement for Basic UDI-DI. Stakeholders should therefore read these three documents as a coherent whole rather than in isolation.

## 3. Impact Analysis

### 3.1 For Original Manufacturers

Original manufacturers need to revisit their commercial and quality agreements with distributors and importers. In practice, many manufacturers treat label changes in the distribution chain as “exceptions” or “tacitly permitted,” without realising that once a distributor triggers Article 16, the original manufacturer no longer bears manufacturer responsibility for the modified device version, but remains responsible for the original device placed on the market before modification. MDCG 2026-5 clarifies the liability cut-off point: **the original manufacturer’s obligations end with the device version corresponding to its original UDI-DI; once a distributor triggers Article 16, all manufacturer obligations for the new version transfer to the distributor.** Manufacturers should include contractual clauses requiring distributors to give written notice before engaging in any activity that may trigger Article 16, and retain original device technical documentation and UDI data to avoid evidentiary gaps in future enforcement.

### 3.2 For Importers and Distributors

For importers and distributors engaged in relabelling, repackaging, private labelling, or parallel trade, MDCG 2026-5 means a significant increase in compliance costs. Once these entities trigger Article 16, they must establish or improve a quality management system meeting Article 10(9) MDR requirements, appoint a qualified person responsible for regulatory compliance (PRRC), prepare technical documentation, implement post-market surveillance, and take charge of UDI assignment and EUDAMED registration. For small and medium-sized enterprises operating on a light-asset trade model, this may present substantial organisational and resource challenges. Failure to comply, however, will be treated as placing a non-compliant device on the market, exposing the entity to administrative penalties, product recalls, and even criminal liability under national law.

Furthermore, Article 16(2) and (3) require importers/distributors assuming manufacturer obligations to inform the original manufacturer and the competent authority of their Member State before carrying out the relevant activities. MDCG 2026-5 does not change these procedural obligations, but stresses that UDI assignment is an integral part of them.

### 3.3 For the Industry Ecosystem

From an industry perspective, MDCG 2026-5 helps eliminate divergences in enforcement among Member States and reduces trade frictions caused by unclear UDI assignment. However, it may also increase compliance friction in intra-EU parallel trade: some parallel traders may reduce translation label operations to avoid manufacturer obligations and instead use original labels with language information leaflets, though whether this approach is consistently accepted remains to be seen. In the long term, clear responsibility boundaries contribute to a credible UDI database, laying a solid foundation for full life-cycle traceability of medical devices.

### 3.4 For Patients and Users

For patients and users, clarified UDI assignment responsibilities mean stronger safety guarantees. When a device changes legal manufacturer identity due to relabelling or private label sales, the new UDI-DI ensures that in the event of adverse events or recalls, regulators and healthcare institutions can accurately identify the responsible party, rapidly determine the affected device scope, and avoid traceability delays or omissions caused by identification confusion.

## 4. Compliance Recommendations

### 4.1 Original Manufacturers

1. **Review distribution agreements**: Explicitly prohibit or restrict distributors/importers from relabelling, repackaging, or selling under their own names without prior written notice and consent.
2. **Establish a UDI assignment matrix**: Record the UDI-DI and Basic UDI-DI for each device version, along with a list of all authorised distributors and importers; regularly compare market feedback to detect unauthorised label changes.
3. **Preserve original data**: Ensure original UDI-DI and EUDAMED registration data remain complete and retrievable, and retain technical documentation proving compliance of the original device even after a distributor triggers Article 16.
4. **Conduct joint training with distributors**: Communicate the requirements of MDCG 2026-5 to the distribution network, clarifying which activities may trigger manufacturer obligations, to reduce compliance blind spots in the supply chain.

### 4.2 Importers/Distributors

1. **Conduct a gap analysis**: Assess whether your business activities (relabelling, repackaging, private label, parallel trade) trigger manufacturer obligations under Article 16 MDR/IVDR.
2. **Build manufacturer capability**: If triggering activities are present, establish a QMS meeting Article 10 MDR/IVDR requirements, appoint a PRRC, prepare technical documentation, and consider whether to rely on Article 16(4) to use the original manufacturer’s conformity assessment.
3. **Assign new UDI-DI and Basic UDI-DI**: For device versions placed on the market after triggering Article 16, assign new UDI-DIs and Basic UDI-DIs through an EU-recognised issuing entity, in accordance with Annex VI Part C.
4. **Complete EUDAMED registration**: Register as a manufacturer in EUDAMED, submit the new UDI-DI and related device data, and establish mapping between old and new UDI-DIs for traceability.
5. **Fulfil notification obligations**: Notify the original manufacturer and the competent authority of your Member State in writing before carrying out the relevant activities.

### 4.3 Parallel Traders

1. **Prioritise language solutions that do not touch the original label**: Use supplementary information leaflets or outer packaging labels without obscuring the original label and UDI carrier to avoid triggering Article 16.
2. **If covering or replacing the original label is unavoidable**: Immediately initiate manufacturer obligation compliance procedures, assign a new UDI-DI, and notify relevant parties under Article 16.
3. **Keep decision records**: Document the label handling approach for each batch in writing for competent authority inspections.

### 4.4 Internal Processes and Documentation

All affected parties should update internal SOPs to incorporate MDCG 2026-5 requirements into UDI management procedures, supplier management procedures, and label change control procedures. Documentation should include: determination of activities triggering Article 16, new UDI-DI assignment records, EUDAMED submission records, and proof of notification to the original manufacturer. Conduct periodic audits of internal and supply chain partner compliance to ensure continuous adherence to MDR/IVDR traceability requirements.

## 5. Timeline & Key Dates

- **22 July 2026**: MDCG 2026-5 Position Paper published, immediately available for stakeholders’ reference. Although not legally binding, it represents a common EU understanding of this issue.
- **MDR/IVDR UDI mandatory implementation schedule**: Under Article 123(3) MDR and Article 113(3) IVDR, UDI carrier obligations have been applied in phases since 26 May 2021. By 2026, most medical devices and some IVDs are already within the mandatory UDI scope; remaining IVD products (e.g., Class A) must comply by 26 May 2027 at the latest. Manufacturer obligations under Article 16 have been effective since 26 May 2021 (MDR date of application), and therefore apply retroactively to relevant activities.
- **Progressive mandatory use of EUDAMED**: Under Regulation (EU) 2024/1860, EUDAMED modules are being progressively made mandatory. MDCG 2026-4 rev.1 (October 2026) addresses the management of SS(C)P after mandatory use, indicating that mandatory use of the UDI/device registration module has entered the practical phase. Stakeholders should immediately ensure their EUDAMED registration and UDI data submission capabilities to avoid being caught off guard when modules become mandatory.

In summary, although MDCG 2026-5 is concise, it addresses the core controversies of UDI assignment responsibility. Manufacturers, importers, and distributors should treat it as a compliance baseline and immediately initiate internal reviews and process adjustments to ensure full implementation of traceability obligations in the EU market.
