## 1. Policy Background and Significance

On 13 November 2026, the European Commission's Directorate-General for Health and Food Safety (DG SANTE) formally published the complete recording of the webinar on the pilot coordinated assessment held on 6 November 2026. The webinar was open to sponsors and national competent authorities, attracting more than 200 participants, and marked a critical juncture of reflection, consolidation, and promotion for the coordinated assessment pilot program under Regulation (EU) 2017/745 on medical devices (MDR) and Regulation (EU) 2017/746 on in vitro diagnostic medical devices (IVDR).

### 1.1 Institutional Origins: Coordinated Assessment Provisions under the MDR and IVDR

The legal basis for the coordinated assessment mechanism derives from Article 78 of the MDR and Article 74 of the IVDR. These provisions respectively address multi-member state application scenarios for clinical investigations (CI) and performance studies (PS), establishing a novel cross-border collaborative review procedure. Under the previous legal framework (i.e., Directive 93/42/EEC on medical devices and Directive 98/79/EC on in vitro diagnostic medical devices), sponsors intending to conduct clinical investigations in multiple EU Member States were required to submit complete application dossiers separately to the competent authority of each Member State, each of which would independently initiate substantive review. This "one-country-one-review" fragmented model resulted in significant administrative duplication, time delays, and inconsistent review outcomes.

Article 78(3) of the MDR explicitly permits a sponsor, when planning to submit clinical investigation applications in more than one Member State, to propose that one Member State act as the coordinating Member State. The coordinating Member State is responsible for preparing the assessment report and transmitting its conclusions to all concerned Member States. Article 74 of the IVDR establishes a parallel provision for performance studies of in vitro diagnostic devices. The core logic of this institutional design is to enable a single high-quality technical assessment to be shared and adopted by multiple Member States, thereby reducing duplicative work, improving the efficiency of review resources, and shortening the overall time for sponsors to obtain multi-country authorizations.

### 1.2 Launch and Evolution of the Pilot Program

While the coordinated assessment provisions are explicitly set forth in the legal texts, their practical implementation requires deep mutual trust between Member State competent authorities, harmonized technical assessment standards, and operational information exchange procedures. Accordingly, the European Commission promoted the launch of the coordinated assessment pilot program following the full application of the MDR and IVDR. According to information disclosed in this webinar, the pilot program has now been operating for a full two years. During this period, sponsors who completed assessments under the first call shared practical feedback alongside the reporting Member States.

The substantive significance of the pilot program extends far beyond procedural exercise. It represents a critical transitional step in the MDR/IVDR system from "legal texts in force" to "mature enforcement practice." By testing the operational mechanics of coordinated assessment through real application cases, the EU is validating the practical operability of Article 78 of the MDR and Article 74 of the IVDR, identifying institutional bottlenecks, and accumulating cooperation experience among Member States.

### 1.3 Strategic Positioning of the Webinar

This webinar is not merely a routine information dissemination event but a milestone with multiple strategic objectives. First, it provides a systematic retrospective of two years of pilot operations, demonstrating the program's tangible outcomes to industry through the publication of participant feedback. Second, it serves as a recruitment initiative targeting future sponsors, clearly signaling the EU's policy intention to expand pilot participation. Third, by publishing the recording in an open format, it removes time and geographic barriers, enabling manufacturers, notified bodies, and regulatory affairs professionals who did not attend the live event to obtain first-hand procedural information.

For Chinese medical device manufacturers, the publication of this recording holds particular practical value. The experiences accumulated and problems identified by pioneering sponsors over the past two years provide valuable reference coordinates for later entrants. The participation conditions, procedural steps, and timelines addressed in the recording are directly relevant to the strategic planning and resource allocation of Chinese enterprises intending to conduct multi-country clinical investigations or performance studies in the EU.

## 2. Core Changes Analysis

### 2.1 Progressive Clarification of the Coordinated Assessment Operational Architecture

After two years of pilot operation, the procedural framework of coordinated assessment has evolved from legal provisions into operationalizable workflows. Based on information disclosed in the webinar and publicly available documents from previous calls, the core operational architecture of coordinated assessment encompasses the following key elements:

**First, designation of the coordinating Member State.** The sponsor proposes one Member State to serve as the coordinating body in a multi-country application. This proposal is typically based on considerations such as the Member State's review expertise in the relevant device category or technical field, facility of language communication, and adequacy of review resource allocation. Once the procedure is initiated, the coordinating Member State takes the lead in preparing the assessment report and transmitting verified technical assessment conclusions to the other concerned Member States.

**Second, preparation and submission of a single application package.** The sponsor is still required to submit applications to all concerned Member States through the EUDAMED clinical investigation/performance study module or through submission channels prescribed by each Member State. However, under the coordinated assessment model, the core technical dossier review is concentrated in the coordinating Member State, with other Member States relying in principle on the assessment results of the coordinating Member State, while retaining review authority only over country-specific elements such as national ethical requirements, subject protection rules, and compensation arrangements.

**Third, preparation and transmission of the assessment report.** The coordinating Member State conducts in-depth review of the completeness, compliance, and scientific soundness of the application dossier in accordance with the assessment standards and timeline framework prescribed in Article 70 of the MDR, and prepares the assessment report. Once transmitted to all concerned Member States, each Member State decides within the statutory period whether to adopt the assessment conclusions and issue its national decision of authorization (or refusal or conditional authorization).

### 2.2 Substantive Progress in Timeline Compression

One of the most notable outcomes disclosed in this webinar is the participants' confirmation that coordinated assessment has significantly shortened the overall time for multi-country authorizations. Under the MDR/IVDR framework, if a sponsor submits independent applications to each Member State sequentially, each Member State's assessment period is typically 45 days (after the validation period), and the timing of review initiation, depth of review, and requests for supplementary information often vary across Member States, resulting in actual total multi-country authorization timelines that may extend to several months or even over a year.

Under the coordinated assessment model, the coordinating Member State's assessment report serves as the common basis for decision-making by all concerned Member States. Because the need for each Member State to independently conduct substantive technical review is reduced, the internal decision-making time of each Member State after receiving the coordinated assessment report is substantially compressed. Furthermore, coordinated assessment significantly reduces the frequency with which sponsors receive duplicative requests for supplementary information—under the traditional model, different Member States might raise different perspectives or even contradictory queries on the same technical issue, requiring substantial resources from the sponsor to respond individually. Coordinated assessment concentrates technical queries at the coordinating Member State level, enabling a single clarification to address common concerns across multiple Member States.

### 2.3 Institutional Optimization Space Identified in Pilot Feedback

Despite the overall positive feedback, the webinar also candidly discussed areas for improvement identified during pilot operations. Participant feedback focused on three dimensions:

**Harmonization of review standards.** Although coordinated assessment aims to unify technical review approaches, differences persist among Member States in key review parameters such as biocompatibility evaluation, determination of the sufficiency of clinical evidence, and risk management acceptance criteria. The assessment report prepared by the coordinating Member State may be partially incompatible with the established review practices of other Member States in certain technical judgments, leading the latter to initiate supplementary national review when adopting the assessment conclusions. This phenomenon of "discordance within coordination" suggests that the EU needs to further publish refined guidance documents through the Medical Device Coordination Group (MDCG) to harmonize technical review parameters across Member States.

**Interoperability of electronic submission processes.** The usability and functional maturity of the EUDAMED clinical investigation/performance study module during the pilot period still have room for improvement. Some participants reported technical obstacles in cross-border data exchange, including format compatibility issues, attachment upload capacity limitations, and system response delays. While these system-level issues do not constitute legal barriers, they may in practice offset the time savings enabled by coordinated assessment.

**Language and communication efficiency.** Language remains a non-negligible practical challenge in multi-country coordinated assessment. Although technical communication in coordinated assessment is in principle conducted in English, some Member State competent authorities still require certain country-specific documents to be submitted in their national languages within their internal administrative procedures. Sponsors must factor these language-related additional requirements into their resource and timeline planning.

### 2.4 Institutional Signals from Pilot to Routinization

The European Commission clearly conveyed its policy intent to promote the transition of coordinated assessment from pilot status to routine practice during the webinar. This can be clearly identified from the following operational arrangements: the European Commission simultaneously published the Expression of Interest Form for Sponsors and a Questions and Answers (Q&A) document. The design of the Expression of Interest Form means that any sponsor intending to participate in coordinated assessment can formally express their intention through a standardized channel and obtain admission confirmation and procedural guidance from the competent authorities. This indicates that the institutionalization of the pilot program is rapidly advancing.

It is reasonable to expect that, with further accumulation of pilot experience, coordinated assessment will progressively be incorporated into the MDCG's formal guidance document system, forming a standard pathway open to all sponsors. For manufacturers currently maintaining a wait-and-see posture, early engagement in the pilot phase means gaining "first-mover advantages" within the rule-finalization window—not only accumulating interaction experience with competent authorities but also having a certain degree of influence over the final determination of procedural details.

## 3. Impact Analysis

### 3.1 Impact on Sponsors and Manufacturers

The core beneficiaries of the coordinated assessment mechanism are sponsors planning to conduct clinical investigations or performance studies in multiple EU Member States. The practical impact manifests at the following levels:

**Structural reduction in time costs.** Taking a clinical investigation conducted in five Member States as an example, under the traditional model, the sponsor would face five independent review processes, up to five rounds of supplementary information requests, and five separate authorization decisions. Coordinated assessment concentrates the core technical review into a single process, with the coordinating Member State's in-depth assessment producing a reusable assessment report, and the national-level review of each concerned Member State reduced to confirmation of the coordinated assessment conclusions and verification of country-specific elements. For medical device companies sensitive to product launch time windows, this time compression directly translates into improved market competitiveness and return on investment.

**Optimized allocation of compliance resources.** Coordinated assessment enables the sponsor's regulatory affairs team to focus their primary efforts on responding to high-quality technical queries from the coordinating Member State, rather than being dispersed across parallel communications with multiple countries. This means that at the initial stage, sponsors need to invest higher-intensity preparatory work in technical documentation preparation and clinical evidence construction to ensure they can pass the in-depth review of the coordinating Member State. Once the assessment report is approved, the probability of obtaining multi-country authorizations improves significantly.

**Strategic importance of coordinating Member State selection.** The choice of coordinating Member State is no longer a matter of administrative convenience but has risen to a strategic decision. Sponsors need to evaluate candidate coordinating Member States on factors such as: their review experience in the target device category, the efficiency of their interface with the clinical investigation ethics review system, their level of regulatory trust with other concerned Member States, and other relevant factors. An incorrect choice of coordinating Member State may result in the assessment report being partially refused adoption by other Member States, thereby forfeiting the expected benefits of coordinated assessment.

### 3.2 Impact on Notified Bodies and Conformity Assessment

Although the coordinated assessment mechanism directly addresses the Member State authorization stage for clinical investigations and performance studies, it also has an indirect but important impact on the conformity assessment work of notified bodies. Under the MDR and IVDR framework, the sufficiency and quality of clinical evidence are core elements of a notified body's conformity assessment when issuing CE certificates. The high-quality, multi-member-state-accepted clinical investigation/performance study assessment reports generated through coordinated assessment can provide notified bodies with stronger reference points during their clinical evaluation consultation process, reducing repeated interactions between notified bodies and sponsors on the sufficiency of clinical evidence.

Furthermore, the trend toward harmonization of review standards driven by coordinated assessment will also indirectly influence the acceptance thresholds applied by notified bodies in clinical evidence review. As the review approaches of Member State competent authorities gradually converge in multi-country coordination scenarios, the regulatory precedents that notified bodies can cite and rely upon in subsequent conformity assessments will become clearer and more predictable.

### 3.3 Impact on Industry Competitive Dynamics

The maturation of the coordinated assessment mechanism will, to a certain extent, reshape the competitive logic of the medical device industry. Sponsors who can swiftly adapt to the multi-country coordinated application model will gain significant speed-to-market advantages. This is particularly relevant for small and medium-sized medical device enterprises: under the traditional model, such enterprises often lacked the capacity to simultaneously respond to multi-country reviews and therefore chose to enter markets sequentially or abandon certain markets. Coordinated assessment enables them to achieve simultaneous EU-wide market entry at lower compliance costs.

At the same time, this institutional evolution raises the front-end requirements for sponsor technical documentation quality. The in-depth review by the coordinating Member State means that any technical deficiencies or insufficient clinical evidence in the application dossier may be exposed at an early stage and trigger chain reactions across multiple countries. The investment in technical documentation quality and clinical study design during the pre-clinical development phase will determine the success or failure of subsequent multi-country authorizations more directly than ever before.

### 3.4 Impact on Patients and Users

From the perspective of end users, the promotion of the coordinated assessment mechanism carries a clear public health interest dimension. Simultaneous multi-country authorization shortens the time from completion of development to clinical availability for innovative medical devices and in vitro diagnostic products, enabling patients in different EU Member States to gain earlier access to innovative diagnostic and therapeutic solutions with clinical value. This intra-regional equalization of accessibility is also a concrete manifestation of the EU's single market principle in the public health domain. At the same time, the more rigorous harmonized review standards promoted by coordinated assessment also strengthen the consistency of subject protection and quality control requirements for clinical investigations, enhancing the ethical and safety assurance level of multicenter studies under the EU framework.

## 4. Compliance Recommendations

### 4.1 Strategic Planning and Decision Framework for Sponsors

For sponsors planning to initiate multi-country clinical investigations or performance studies in the EU during 2026-2027, the following steps are recommended for priority consideration:

**Step One: Assess the applicability and benefits of coordinated assessment.** Not all multi-country studies are automatically suited to the coordinated assessment model. Sponsors should conduct a cost-benefit analysis taking into account the number of target Member States, device risk classification, complexity of the clinical investigation design, projected timelines, and internal resource capacity. Generally speaking, studies involving more than three Member States and requiring high-depth technical assessment—such as clinical investigations of high-risk implantable devices or performance studies of novel in vitro diagnostic markers—stand to gain the most substantial benefits from coordinated assessment.

**Step Two: Submit the expression of interest as early as possible.** The European Commission has published the Expression of Interest Form for Sponsors. Sponsors intending to participate are advised to establish contact with the potential coordinating Member State competent authority through the expression of interest channel at least 6-9 months before formal application submission, once the clinical investigation protocol is substantially finalized, to understand the specific participation conditions and procedural requirements under the pilot framework for that Member State. Early communication can effectively avoid discovering at the formal submission stage that the protocol design does not align with coordinated assessment requirements.

**Step Three: Carefully select the coordinating Member State.** Selection of the coordinating Member State should be based on a comprehensive evaluation of the following dimensions: (1) the Member State's review experience and technical expertise in the intended device category and clinical indication; (2) the depth of the Member State competent authority's participation in the coordinated assessment pilot and its past performance; (3) the regulatory cooperation relationships and mutual recognition record between the Member State and other target Member States; (4) language communication convenience and competent authority responsiveness; and (5) consistency with the sponsor's existing regulatory relationships or the location of the Authorized Representative.

### 4.2 Reinforcement Strategy for Technical Documentation Preparation

The coordinated assessment model imposes "concentrated" and "deepened" characteristics on the quality requirements for application dossiers. The review by a single coordinating Member State will serve as the basis for decision-making by all participating Member States, and sponsors should therefore raise documentation quality to a level capable of passing the most stringent review of any Member State before submission.

**Scientific rigor of the clinical investigation protocol.** Sponsors should introduce multidisciplinary review by biostatisticians, clinical experts, and the regulatory affairs team at the protocol design stage to ensure that key elements—including endpoint selection, sample size calculation, statistical analysis methods, and endpoint adjudication standards—comply with the clinical evidence requirements of Article 62 and Annex XV of the MDR. The expert reviewers of the coordinating Member State will conduct in-depth interrogation of these elements, and any logical gaps in the protocol design may be amplified in the coordinated assessment.

**Sufficiency and traceability of preclinical data.** For high-risk devices, the application dossier should provide complete and traceable preclinical evaluation data, including biocompatibility test reports, bench test data, animal study data (where applicable), and risk management documentation. The coordinating Member State will typically require that these data establish a clear logical connection with the indication scope, target population, and risk control strategy of the study protocol during its review.

**Advance identification of country-specific elements.** Although coordinated assessment concentrates on technical review, each concerned Member State retains review authority over country-specific elements, including ethics committee approval, national language versions of informed consent documents, damage compensation and insurance arrangements, and data protection compliance. Sponsors should prepare country-specific documentation packages for all concerned Member States in parallel with the coordinated assessment application submission, to avoid unnecessary delays after the coordinated assessment is approved due to incomplete country-level elements.

### 4.3 Coordination with Notified Bodies and the EU Authorized Representative

While planning participation in coordinated assessment, sponsors should maintain close coordination with the notified body and the EU Authorized Representative. For devices already in or planned to enter the conformity assessment procedure, there is a relationship between the coordinated assessment results for clinical investigations/performance studies and the notified body's review of clinical evidence during the CE certification stage. Sponsors should ensure that the assessment reports, supplementary information exchange records, and final authorization decisions generated during the coordinated assessment are properly archived and incorporated into the clinical evaluation documentation package required for notified body conformity assessment.

For non-EU manufacturers, the EU Authorized Representative plays an indispensable role in the coordinated assessment procedure. The Authorized Representative not only handles formal correspondence with competent authorities but should also serve as a local regulatory intelligence function during the strategic planning and documentation preparation phases of coordinated assessment, providing practical advice on coordinating Member State selection, procedural expectations, and local administrative practices.

### 4.4 Risk Monitoring and Contingency Planning

While coordinated assessment significantly reduces overall timeline risk, it also introduces new concentrated risks. If the coordinating Member State's assessment report reaches a negative conclusion, or if the coordinating Member State raises major technical objections during the review, the associated negative results may rapidly propagate to all participating Member States. Therefore, sponsors should establish contingency response plans containing the following elements before entering the coordinated assessment procedure:

- Response strategies and escalation pathways for major technical objections encountered during coordinated assessment;
- Procedural preparations for transitioning to an independent application in a Member State that refuses to adopt the coordinated assessment conclusions;
- Impact assessment and adjustment plans for the overall clinical investigation program and product launch timeline in the event of delays in the coordinated assessment schedule.
