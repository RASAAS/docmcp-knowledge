## 1. Policy Background & Significance

### 1.1 Market Availability Concerns During the MDR/IVDR Transition

Since the Medical Devices Regulation (Regulation (EU) 2017/745, "MDR") became applicable on 26 May 2021, and the In Vitro Diagnostic Medical Devices Regulation (Regulation (EU) 2017/746, "IVDR") became applicable on 26 May 2022, the European medical device regulatory system has undergone its most profound paradigm shift in decades. The new Regulations significantly raise clinical evidence requirements, strengthen the designation and oversight standards for Notified Bodies (NBs), and substantially expand post-market surveillance and vigilance obligations. However, this stringent regulatory framework has also generated widespread structural anxiety in practice: is the pace and capacity of Notified Body designation sufficient to absorb the large backlog of certificates requiring transition? Do small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs) have sufficient resources to complete the upgrading of their technical documentation? Will certain devices—particularly low-volume, high-clinical-value niche products—be forced to exit the EU market due to disproportionate compliance costs?

To address these concerns, the EU legislature successively adopted Regulation (EU) 2023/607 and Regulation (EU) 2024/1860, extending the MDR and IVDR transition periods respectively, and introducing Article 10a into both MDR and IVDR. Article 10a requires manufacturers to notify relevant competent authorities, notified bodies (where applicable), economic operators, and health institutions at least six months before an anticipated interruption or discontinuation of supply. The core purpose of this "supply interruption alert" mechanism is to safeguard device availability for patients and users, preventing transition-period compliance pressure from materialising into an actual clinical supply crisis.

However, the establishment of legal obligations does not automatically generate regulatory insight. The European Commission urgently needed an empirical foundation to systematically assess the actual impact of the MDR/IVDR transition on device market access—which device categories are declining, which Member States or product areas face shortage risks, where Notified Body capacity bottlenecks exist, and what obstacles are being encountered by economic operators and healthcare service providers respectively. It was for this reason that the European Commission's Directorate-General for Health and Food Safety (DG SANTE), through the European Health and Digital Executive Agency (HaDEA), commissioned a dedicated study: the "Study supporting the monitoring of availability of medical devices on the EU market."

### 1.2 Genesis of the Study and the Dashboard

The study commenced in December 2022 with a duration of 36 months (until December 2025). It was contracted to a consortium led by the Austrian National Public Health Institute (Gesundheit Österreich GmbH/GÖG), in collaboration with Areté and Civic Consulting. One of the core deliverables of the study is the medical device availability monitoring dashboard developed for the European Commission. The dashboard consolidates survey data gathered from different stakeholders and integrates comparable data from previous surveys of Notified Bodies conducted by the European Commission.

On 14 July 2026, Dashboard version 3.5 was officially released. This version marks a significant methodological and data-granularity leap forward: it achieves for the first time the systematic integration of three rounds of economic operator surveys (end of 2023, end of 2024, end of 2025) and the healthcare service provider survey (2024), and through enhanced data visualisation functions, provides manufacturers, Notified Bodies, and competent authorities with more intuitive trend analysis and shortage alert capabilities.

### 1.3 Regulatory Significance of Dashboard v3.5

The release of Dashboard v3.5 is not an isolated IT update. It embodies the European Commission's regulatory approach of managing MDR/IVDR transition risks through data-driven means: moving from "passively responding to shortage reports" towards "proactively identifying structural market access risks." For manufacturers, authorised representatives, Notified Bodies, competent authorities, and even healthcare service providers and procurement bodies, the trend data presented by this dashboard is gradually becoming a "factual benchmark" running parallel to the legal texts. While it does not directly impose additional legal obligations, it provides an independent empirical reference for triggering Article 10a supply interruption notifications, Notified Body capacity planning, market surveillance prioritisation by national competent authorities, and risk-based sourcing decisions by procurement bodies.

From a broader perspective, the monitoring capability represented by Dashboard v3.5 is an extension of the EU's health security architecture—particularly the work of the Health Emergency Preparedness and Response Authority (HERA)—into the medical device sector. It elevates device availability from being merely a commercial or compliance issue to being embedded within the policy framework of public health and supply security.

## 2. Core Changes Analysis

### 2.1 Integration of the Third Economic Operator Survey (End of 2025)

One of the most central content updates in Dashboard v3.5 is the incorporation of the third round of economic operator survey results conducted at the end of 2025. Compared with the first survey targeting manufacturers and authorised representatives at the end of 2023 and the second economic operator survey at the end of 2024, the key value of the third survey lies in its longitudinal depth: it constitutes a continuous data chain spanning two years, enabling the European Commission and stakeholders to observe—for the first time in time-series form—the following critical changes:

- Market exit and new entry trends for devices of different risk classes (MDR Class I to III; IVDR Class A to D);
- The direction of supply fluctuations for specific product categories (e.g., surgical instruments, implants, diagnostic reagents);
- The evolution of the time gap between Notified Body certificate transition progress and manufacturer application submissions;
- Differences in compliance advancement speed between SMEs and larger enterprises.

This longitudinal data is of irreplaceable value for understanding the actual implementation quality of the extended transition periods (MDR to 31 December 2027 / 31 December 2028; IVDR staged through 2027–2029). It provides, for the first time, a quantifiable evidence base for the critical policy question of whether the transition period extensions have genuinely alleviated market exit pressure.

### 2.2 Inclusion of Healthcare Service Provider Survey Data (2024)

Dashboard v3.5 adds a data dimension that was relatively weak in previous versions: direct feedback from healthcare service providers. The survey conducted in 2024 covered health professionals, health institutions, and medical device procurement bodies, and its data reflects the real-world experience at the clinical end of the supply chain regarding device availability, including:

- Changes in the availability of specific device categories in clinical use;
- Supply delays, stock-outs, or substitutability problems encountered during procurement;
- Adjustments in inventory and back-up sourcing strategies by healthcare institutions;
- Expectations regarding supply risks during the remainder of the transition period.

The cross-presentation of clinical end-point data with upstream economic operator data is a notable methodological advancement in Dashboard v3.5. It breaks through the traditional limitation of regulatory data being heavily weighted toward the supply side while neglecting the user side, making the verification and localisation of shortage signals more precise. For example, when manufacturer exit data for a given device category and hospital procurement delay data rise simultaneously, the dashboard can provide a more persuasive indication of causation.

### 2.3 Enhanced Data Visualisation Functions

Dashboard v3.5 has undergone substantive enhancements at the user interface and data presentation levels. According to the official description, this version provides stakeholders—including manufacturers, Notified Bodies, and competent authorities—with "enhanced data visualisation," specifically embodied in the following:

- **Improved interactivity of trend charts**: users can filter and drill down along multiple dimensions, including timeline, device category, risk class, Member State, or stakeholder type;
- **Intuitive shortage warning indicators**: potential shortage risks are no longer presented solely in raw data form but are visually stratified through colour-coding and risk-level labelling;
- **Side-by-side display of historical comparative data**: the dashboard integrates survey data previously collected by the European Commission from Notified Bodies, enabling users to compare Notified Body capacity data with economic operator supply data on the same interface and identify structural mismatches;
- **Optimised export and reporting functions**: facilitating the embedding of dashboard data into internal regulatory compliance annual reviews and market surveillance reporting processes by regulators and companies alike.

### 2.4 Integration of Historical Notified Body Survey Data for Comparability

Dashboard v3.5 continues and strengthens the integration of Notified Body survey data. The study incorporates the results of previous surveys of Notified Bodies conducted by the European Commission into the same analytical framework, enabling direct comparison between supply-side data—such as the certification capacity, review timelines, and application rejection rates of MDR/IVDR-designated Notified Bodies—and demand-side data from economic operators, including submission rates, certificate transition rates, and market exit intentions. This integration has significant policy diagnostic value: it can reveal the "capacity-demand gap" during a given period and provide a data foundation for the Commission to assess whether further adjustments to transition arrangements or additional support measures (such as prioritising additional Notified Body designations or improving technical documentation review efficiency) are needed.

### 2.5 Systematic Multi-Stakeholder Coverage

Taken together, Dashboard v3.5 completes a panoramic monitoring system covering the following actors:

| Data Source | Survey Timing | Coverage |
|---|---|---|
| First Economic Operator Survey | End of 2023 | Manufacturers, Authorised Representatives |
| Healthcare Service Provider Survey | 2024 | Health professionals, health institutions, procurement bodies |
| Second Economic Operator Survey | End of 2024 | Economic operators |
| Third Economic Operator Survey | End of 2025 | Economic operators |
| Historical Notified Body Surveys (EC) | Prior | Notified Bodies |

The significance of this system is that the European Commission has, for the first time, achieved interactive, visualised comparison of data from the three critical links of the device supply chain—certification supply (Notified Bodies), product supply (manufacturers / authorised representatives / economic operators), and clinical demand (healthcare providers and procurers)—on a single unified platform. This is a foundational achievement in evidence-based medical device supply security governance in the MDR/IVDR era.

## 3. Impact Analysis

### 3.1 Impact on Manufacturers and Authorised Representatives

For manufacturers and authorised representatives, Dashboard v3.5 serves both as an external benchmarking tool and as a compliance early-warning signal source.

**First, a calibration benchmark for market exit decisions.** The category-specific and risk-class-specific supply trends presented in the dashboard provide an external reference for manufacturers to assess the regulatory sustainability of their own product portfolios. If the market-wide exit rate for a given product category rises significantly while the manufacturer's own products in that category have not yet completed MDR/IVDR transition, the dashboard data effectively constitutes a "silent signal" prompting management to accelerate decisions: continue investing resources to complete certification, or plan an orderly market exit and initiate the Article 10a notification process.

**Second, auxiliary judgment for Article 10a supply interruption notifications.** Under MDR Article 10a and IVDR Article 10a, manufacturers must notify relevant competent authorities, notified bodies (where applicable), economic operators, and health institutions at least six months before an anticipated interruption or discontinuation of supply. The shortage trend data provided by the dashboard can help manufacturers identify whether a "systemic supply interruption risk" exists—when supply data for comparable products across the market show synchronous decline, the manufacturer's own capacity fluctuations are more likely to be regarded as part of a systemic problem, thereby strengthening the necessity of early notification.

**Third, indirect responsibility for survey participation and data quality.** The reliability of the dashboard depends on survey participation rates and data authenticity. Although survey participation is not itself a legal obligation, competent authorities may well refer to the dashboard data when conducting market surveillance and assessing the adequacy of Article 10a notifications. Manufacturers should be aware that the consistency between their disclosures in surveys and their subsequent statements in regulatory interactions will face increasingly rigorous scrutiny.

### 3.2 Impact on Notified Bodies

Notified Bodies constitute the critical supply node during the MDR/IVDR transition period. The impact of Dashboard v3.5 on them is reflected in the following:

- **An empirical foundation for capacity planning**: Notified Bodies can utilise the demand-side data in the dashboard (the volume, timing, and category distribution of manufacturer applications) to optimise their own audit resource allocation, prioritising product categories with high clinical urgency or elevated market exit risk;
- **Performance pressure arising from transparency**: the inclusion of historical Notified Body survey data means that review timelines, rejection rates, and capacity utilisation indicators of individual Notified Bodies are compared and scrutinised within a broader context, which will indirectly drive Notified Bodies to improve audit efficiency and consistency;
- **Coordinated early warning with competent authorities**: when the dashboard shows a rapid decline in supply for a certain category while Notified Body certification applications in that category also decline synchronously, Notified Bodies can proactively communicate with competent authorities to explore accelerated pathways or transition-period support measures.

### 3.3 Overall Impact on Industry

For the medical device industry as a whole, the release of Dashboard v3.5 reinforces a long-term trend: **compliance transparency is extending from "outcome-oriented" (whether a certificate has been obtained) to "process-oriented" (what is the transition progress, and where do supply risks lie).** This means:

- Industry associations and trade organisations need to more systematically incorporate dashboard data into their guidance to members, helping SMEs move from "passively waiting for certificates" to "proactively monitoring market signals";
- Due diligence in investment and M&A transactions will increasingly cite such publicly available monitoring data as an external basis for assessing the transition-period risk of a target company's device portfolio;
- The structural shortage risks revealed by the dashboard may constitute trigger conditions for the European Commission to adopt further legislative or policy interventions in the future (such as targeted transition-period adjustments for specific product categories, or directed expansion of Notified Body capacity).

### 3.4 Impact on Patients, Users, and Procurement Bodies

For patients, clinical users, and procurement bodies, Dashboard v3.5 provides, for the first time, a publicly accessible, cross-Member State view of device supply trends. Procurement bodies can accordingly:

- Pre-assess the supply disruption risk of specific product categories in tenders and centralised procurement contracts, and include multi-sourcing clauses;
- For devices on which there is high clinical dependence but for which market exit signals are evident, proactively initiate substitute evaluation or adjust inventory alert thresholds;
- Interface dashboard data with in-house hospital consumable management information systems to establish institution-level device availability warning mechanisms.

From a patient safety perspective, the supply transparency promoted by the dashboard ultimately serves one core objective: avoiding the clinical risks of "no device available" or "forced use of non-optimal substitutes" resulting from transition-period compliance challenges.

## 4. Compliance Recommendations

### 4.1 Recommended Actions for Manufacturers and Authorised Representatives

1. **Immediately review Dashboard v3.5 data and conduct a portfolio benchmarking exercise.** Compare product lines that have completed MDR/IVDR certification, are under application, have not yet initiated application, or are planned for market exit against dashboard trend data by device category. Identify high-risk products where the market-wide exit rate is rising but the company has not yet initiated transition.

2. **Establish an internal dashboard monitoring mechanism.** Designate the regulatory affairs or supply chain management function to track dashboard updates on a regular basis (quarterly is recommended), and incorporate key changes into management review agendas, with particular attention to category trends directly relevant to the company's portfolio.

3. **Refine internal trigger processes for Article 10a supply interruption notifications.** Using dashboard data as an important reference, establish quantified notification trigger criteria (e.g., market-wide supply in a given category declines for two consecutive survey cycles beyond a defined threshold, and the company's own supply in that category also shows a declining trend), and incorporate these into the quality management system's change and supply interruption management procedures.

4. **Actively cooperate with future surveys.** If subsequent survey requests are received from the European Commission, HaDEA, or the research consortium, ensure data authenticity and consistent reporting, and designate personnel with cross-functional visibility to coordinate responses, avoiding single-department provision of partial data.

5. **Use dashboard data to support transition resource prioritisation decisions.** Where CE certificate transition resources are limited, prioritise products with high clinical irreplaceability, high market supply concentration, and elevated shortage risk as evidenced by the dashboard.

### 4.2 Recommended Actions for Notified Bodies

1. **Incorporate dashboard demand-side data into annual audit resource planning.** Dynamically adjust the disciplinary composition of technical documentation review teams based on application submission trends and category distribution.

2. **Proactively notify competent authorities of structural risks.** If a sharp decline in applications for a particular category is identified or cross-corroborated with market exit data, proactively communicate with the relevant competent authorities to explore the possibility of accelerated review or joint handling.

3. **Review how historical survey data are presented in the dashboard and verify data consistency.** Where discrepancies with previously submitted data are identified, trace the causes and prepare explanations in anticipation of potential inquiries from the Commission or competent authorities.

### 4.3 Recommended Actions for Competent Authorities

1. **Cross-validate dashboard data with Article 10a notification information.** When receiving a manufacturer's supply interruption or discontinuation notification, compare it against category trends in the dashboard to determine whether the notification forms part of a broader structural market problem, and decide accordingly whether coordinated action at national or EU level is warranted.

2. **Use the dashboard to identify priority market surveillance targets.** For device categories showing rapid supply decline or concentrated exits in the dashboard, strengthen post-market surveillance and vigilance data review for those products.

3. **Contribute to subsequent data inputs and feedback loops.** As the core enforcement actors in MDR/IVDR implementation, competent authorities' data feedback—particularly information related to Notified Body designation and oversight—plays a crucial role in the continued improvement of the dashboard.

### 4.4 Recommended Actions for Healthcare Service Providers and Procurement Bodies

1. **Incorporate the dashboard into institutional device supply risk reviews.** It is recommended that procurement departments use the latest dashboard version as a baseline before each procurement year to identify high-risk product categories and formulate back-up sourcing plans.

2. **Preset supply interruption response clauses in centralised procurement and framework agreements.** These may include pre-evaluation of substitutes, safety stock requirements, and periodic supplier reporting obligations.

3. **Continue participation in healthcare service provider surveys.** Authentic feedback from healthcare institutions and the procurement end is the foundation of dashboard data quality; relevant personnel should be encouraged to value and accurately complete such surveys.

### 4.5 Recommended Actions for Industry Associations and Trade Organisations

1. **Conduct dashboard interpretation training for members.** Help SMEs understand the regulatory implications of dashboard data to avoid compliance decision delays caused by information asymmetry.

2. **Consolidate member-level common risks and feed them back to the Commission.** Combine dashboard data with concrete obstacles encountered by members in practice to formulate well-evidenced policy recommendations.

It should be particularly noted that although Dashboard v3.5 was released after the conclusion of the study period (December 2025), as the core deliverable of the final phase of the study it carries the integration and presentation of data collected over the entire 36-month research period. Stakeholders should not regard it as a static endpoint, but rather as the instrumental foundation for the European Commission's continued monitoring of device availability and dynamic assessment of MDR/IVDR transition impacts. Going forward, as the Commission's attention deepens on the post-transition market landscape, the multi-source data integration methodology established by this dashboard is highly likely to be continued or extended into a broader health supply security monitoring system.

**Conclusion**: Dashboard v3.5 is an important milestone in the European Commission's use of empirical data to manage MDR/IVDR transition risks. For all market participants subject to MDR/IVDR, it is not merely a visualisation tool, but a strategic early-warning system. Stakeholders should integrate it into their day-to-day compliance planning and supply security management practices, using data as an anchor to proactively address the dual challenges of compliance and supply chain resilience in the final phase of the transition period.
