## 1. Policy Background and Significance

On 3 November 2026, the European Union's Medical Device Coordination Group (MDCG) published MDCG 2026-6, dedicated guidance on clinical evidence requirements for orphan in vitro diagnostic medical devices (orphan IVDs) under Regulation (EU) 2017/746 (IVDR). This is the first EU-level harmonised document to systematically address clinical evidence for orphan IVDs, aiming to help manufacturers and notified bodies demonstrate that a product meets the scientific validity, analytical performance and clinical performance requirements of Article 56 and Annex XIII of the IVDR even when the patient population is extremely small.

Since the IVDR became applicable on 26 May 2022, all in vitro diagnostic medical devices have faced significantly stronger clinical evidence requirements than under the former IVDD. Under Article 56(1) of the IVDR, manufacturers must demonstrate conformity with the general safety and performance requirements in Annex I through performance evaluation. Article 56(2) further requires that the performance evaluation be based on scientific validity, analytical performance and clinical performance. Article 56(3) requires clinical evidence to support the intended purpose, and Part A of Annex XIII sets out the detailed content of the performance evaluation. For orphan IVDs, however, meeting these requirements presents structural difficulties: the target disease is rare, available samples are scarce, and large prospective clinical performance studies are usually not feasible. For a long time, manufacturers lacked clear guidance on evidence strategies, and notified bodies applied inconsistent review standards, causing some orphan IVDs to be delayed or even withdrawn from the IVDR certification pathway.

The publication of MDCG 2026-6 fills this regulatory gap. The guidance does not lower the statutory safety baseline, but instead provides a risk-proportionate, evidence-tiered and real-world-data-oriented compliance pathway for orphan IVDs. Its core significance is fourfold. First, it harmonises the basic logic for reviewing orphan IVD clinical evidence at EU level, reducing divergent interpretations among notified bodies. Second, it recognises the unique evidence-generation challenges of orphan IVDs and permits prioritisation of alternative evidence sources. Third, it guides manufacturers to build a continuous evidence-generation loop between pre-market and post-market settings, strengthening post-market performance follow-up (PMPF). Fourth, it promotes access to diagnostics for rare diseases while safeguarding patient safety and diagnostic accuracy. Manufacturers preparing IVDR technical documentation, seeking notified body conformity assessment, or undergoing surveillance audits must immediately review this guidance and adjust their performance evaluation plans and technical documentation.

## 2. Core Changes Analysis

### 2.1 Definition of Orphan IVDs: Operational Criteria rather than a New Legal Classification

MDCG 2026-6 does not create a new statutory category outside the IVDR, but instead sets out operational criteria for identifying an orphan IVD. The spirit of the guidance is that the manufacturer should first demonstrate that, because the target patient population is very small, samples are scarce, or the disease is rare, generating clinical evidence through conventional pathways is not feasible or is severely restricted. Key factors include the prevalence or incidence of the target disease, the size of the population covered by the intended purpose, the number of obtainable positive samples, the availability of alternative diagnostic products, the natural history of the disease, and the unmet clinical need. The guidance stresses that orphan status is not a unilateral claim by the manufacturer; it must be objectively documented in the technical documentation through epidemiological data, market analysis and sampling feasibility assessments. Notified bodies will focus on whether that justification is sufficient and logically coherent.

### 2.2 Recalibration of the Clinical Evidence Strategy: Risk Proportionality as the Core Principle

The IVDR does not allow exemption from clinical evidence obligations for orphan IVDs, but MDCG 2026-6 makes clear that the depth and breadth of clinical evidence should be proportionate to the device risk, intended purpose and residual risk. For lower-risk orphan IVDs or those used for rule-out diagnosis, the evidence package may rely more heavily on analytical performance data, published clinical association literature and expert consensus. For high-risk orphan IVDs used for definitive diagnosis or treatment decisions, manufacturers should still provide as robust clinical performance data as possible, supplemented by rigorous post-market follow-up. Manufacturers should develop a risk matrix, identify the consequences of false-positive and false-negative results, and link evidence gaps to risk control measures. This principle is consistent with the general safety and performance requirements in Annex I of the IVDR, but the guidance makes it concrete in the orphan IVD setting.

### 2.3 Scientific Validity as the Critical Starting Point

Scientific validity is the first anchor in the clinical evidence chain for an orphan IVD. MDCG 2026-6 requires manufacturers to systematically collect and evaluate evidence linking the measurand to a specific disease state. For known genetic variants, biomarkers or pathogen targets, scientific validity may be relatively mature and can be demonstrated through systematic literature reviews, clinical guidelines, international databases and expert opinion. For newly discovered markers or those reported only in small samples, the guidance cautions that the strength of evidence must be carefully assessed, and analytical performance data alone cannot be used to infer clinical significance. Manufacturers should establish a traceable literature search protocol, inclusion and exclusion criteria, quality appraisal methods and an evidence synthesis report to ensure compliance with Section 1 of Part A of Annex XIII.

### 2.4 Real-World Data and Registries Formally Enter the Evidence Hierarchy

The most notable pragmatic shift in MDCG 2026-6 is the explicit recognition of the value of real-world data (RWD) and real-world evidence (RWE) for orphan IVD clinical evidence. Because prospective studies are often infeasible, the guidance encourages manufacturers to use data from patient registries, biobanks, multicentre collaborative studies, international rare disease consortia, residual samples from previous clinical trials, medical records and laboratory testing databases. However, the guidance also stresses that not all real-world data automatically qualify as acceptable clinical evidence. Manufacturers must clearly assess data relevance, completeness, quality, traceability, bias control and the statistical analysis plan. When data come from third-party registry systems, manufacturers should describe data governance, ethics approval and patient informed consent arrangements to ensure compliance with the GDPR and applicable law.

### 2.5 Design Flexibility and Acceptable Alternatives for Clinical Performance Studies

MDCG 2026-6 acknowledges that traditional prospective multicentre studies are often not feasible for orphan IVDs. The guidance therefore allows more flexible clinical performance study designs, including retrospective sample testing, use of longitudinal sample banks, enrichment designs, adaptive designs, international multicentre collaboration, and case-control designs. For diseases with extremely low prevalence, a limited number of well-characterised samples may be used, supplemented by expert judgment and post-market follow-up. The guidance requires that, regardless of the design chosen, a study protocol and statistical analysis plan be predefined, and that the primary clinical performance endpoints, sample size justification, bias control measures and missing data handling rules be clearly specified. Study conclusions must support the intended purpose claims, and analytical performance must not be simply extrapolated to clinical performance without adequate data.

### 2.6 New Expectations for Technical Documentation and the Performance Evaluation Report

MDCG 2026-6 imposes higher-level presentation requirements for technical documentation, especially the performance evaluation report. The report should not merely list data, but should clearly present the justification of orphan IVD status, evidence gap analysis, explanation of alternative evidence sources, risk proportionality reasoning, residual risk assessment, and plans for post-market data generation. Notified bodies will focus on whether orphan status is established, whether evidence gaps are fully identified, whether alternative evidence is acceptable, and whether the PMPF plan is sufficient to resolve uncertainty within a reasonable time. Under the technical documentation obligations in Article 10 of the IVDR, manufacturers should integrate the logic of MDCG 2026-6 into the performance evaluation report rather than presenting fragmented, disconnected evidence.

## 3. Impact Analysis

### 3.1 Impact on Manufacturers

MDCG 2026-6 brings significantly clearer regulatory expectations for orphan IVD manufacturers, but also higher documentation quality requirements. Manufacturers that continue to use standard IVD evidence templates are likely to face obstacles in notified body reviews. The new guidance requires manufacturers to justify orphan status early in the project, identify feasible evidence pathways, and establish cross-functional cooperation involving regulatory affairs, clinical research, biostatistics, data management and medical affairs. For products already in the certification process or already certified, manufacturers must assess whether existing technical documentation meets the spirit of the new guidance and complete gap remediation before the next surveillance audit or certificate renewal. For products still in development, real-world data collection and registry participation should be incorporated into the clinical evidence plan as early as possible to avoid an insufficient pre-market evidence package.

### 3.2 Impact on the Industry

At the industry level, MDCG 2026-6 is expected to reduce divergent review practices among notified bodies and improve the predictability of orphan IVD certification, thereby attracting more resources to rare disease diagnostics. The value of registries, biobanks and collaborative networks will increase further, and organisations with data governance and real-world evidence capabilities will gain competitive advantages. At the same time, the guidance will drive methodological development in regulatory science for orphan IVDs, such as small-sample statistical methods, evidence synthesis techniques and post-market evidence-generation models. Some small and medium-sized diagnostic companies may face greater pressure due to lack of real-world data resources and regulatory capability, which may accelerate industry consolidation and technical collaboration.

### 3.3 Impact on Patients and Users

For rare disease patients and clinical laboratory users, the positive impact of MDCG 2026-6 is that more orphan IVDs are likely to obtain IVDR certification within a reasonable timeframe, reducing the risk of reduced diagnostic access due to insufficient evidence. While permitting flexible evidence pathways, the guidance still upholds risk proportionality and post-market follow-up principles, so patients can gain faster access while remaining protected by continuous PMPF data. However, patient organisations and clinicians should also monitor the transparency of alternative evidence pathways to ensure that limited pre-market data do not obscure performance changes in real-world use.

## 4. Compliance Recommendations

### 4.1 Conduct an Immediate Evidence Gap Assessment

Manufacturers should immediately convene regulatory, clinical and statistical teams to evaluate existing performance evaluation reports, scientific validity data, analytical performance data, clinical performance data and technical documentation against Article 56 of the IVDR, Part A of Annex XIII, and MDCG 2026-6. The assessment should focus on whether orphan status justification is sufficient, whether real-world data are traceable, and whether clinical performance claims are supported. The gap assessment results should be documented in a written report and converted into a remediation plan.

### 4.2 Rebuild the Clinical Evidence Plan

For orphan IVDs that have not yet been submitted or are preparing for conformity assessment, the clinical evidence plan should be re-developed. The plan should specify the justification for orphan IVD status, the types of evidence available, the alternative strategies to be used, real-world data sources, clinical performance study design, and the post-market data generation pathway. The plan should be executable and set clear data quality thresholds and statistical analysis requirements.

### 4.3 Strengthen Scientific Validity Evidence Management

A systematic literature search and evidence appraisal process should be established. The search strategy should include rare disease databases, clinical guidelines, peer-reviewed literature and international conference abstracts. Literature screening should follow predefined inclusion and exclusion criteria, and evidence quality should be graded. The scientific validity conclusion should clearly support the association between the measurand and the clinical condition, and should not rely solely on a small number of case reports.

### 4.4 Build Collaborative Networks and Registry Channels

Manufacturers should proactively join or help establish rare disease diagnostic registries, sample banks and expert networks. Cooperation with rare disease alliances, academic centres and patient organisations can provide high-quality retrospective samples and real-world data. For data use, formal data-sharing agreements should be established, covering ethics approval, informed consent, data protection and intellectual property to ensure that data sources are compliant.

### 4.5 Improve Post-Market Performance Follow-up and Periodic Updates

Under Part B of Annex XIII of the IVDR, all IVDs must undergo post-market performance follow-up. For orphan IVDs, PMPF is not only a compliance requirement but also a key mechanism for filling pre-market evidence gaps. Manufacturers should develop a detailed PMPF plan specifying data collection frequency, sample size accumulation targets, statistical methods, performance endpoints, and conditions triggering risk assessment. PMPF reports should be updated periodically, and the latest evidence should be reflected in the performance evaluation report.

### 4.6 Engage Early with Notified Bodies

Because clinical evidence strategies for orphan IVDs are highly case-specific, manufacturers are advised to hold structured pre-submission discussions with the notified body, presenting a summary of the evidence strategy and orphan status justification. Early engagement helps identify review concerns and avoid major changes during the formal assessment. For already certified products, manufacturers should also proactively inform the notified body of technical documentation updates made in line with MDCG 2026-6.

## 5. Timeline and Key Dates

MDCG 2026-6 was published and became effective immediately on 3 November 2026. As a guidance document, it does not have its own transitional period, and notified bodies may use it as a reference from the date of publication. For ongoing conformity assessment projects, manufacturers should supplement orphan status justification and alternative evidence explanations as soon as possible. For already certified products, it is recommended that a technical documentation gap assessment be completed within 12 months after publication, and that remedial actions be reflected in the subsequent surveillance audit, certificate renewal or significant change. For legacy devices still covered by IVDR transitional arrangements, manufacturers should incorporate MDCG 2026-6 requirements into the performance evaluation documentation before applying for IVDR certification, to avoid rushed preparation at the end of the transition period. Stakeholders should continue to monitor future MDCG supplementary Q&A documents or revisions on orphan IVD evidence methods and update internal procedures accordingly.
