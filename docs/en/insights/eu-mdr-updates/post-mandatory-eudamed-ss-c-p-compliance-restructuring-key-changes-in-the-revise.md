The European Commission's Medical Device Coordination Group (MDCG) published a revised position paper on 30 October 2026 concerning the management of Summaries of Safety and Clinical Performance (SSCP, also referred to as SS(C)P) in EUDAMED following the mandatory use of the relevant modules. The revision is addressed to manufacturers of implantable and Class III devices, notified bodies, and other EUDAMED users involved in the submission and validation of SSCPs. It provides a package of clarifications and refinements covering the preparation, upload, validation, publication, and lifecycle management of SSCPs in EUDAMED.

From a legal perspective, the SSCP regime originates from Article 32 of Regulation (EU) 2017/745 (MDR). That article requires manufacturers of implantable devices and Class III devices to draw up a summary of safety and clinical performance, which must be validated by a notified body and then submitted to EUDAMED. An SSCP is not an alternative to the instructions for use (IFU), nor is it a full clinical evaluation report (CER). Rather, it is a publicly available summary of safety and clinical performance information, validated by a notified body, intended for patients, users, and healthcare professionals. Its content should include device identification, intended purpose, indications, contraindications, residual risks, clinical performance data, and post-market clinical follow-up (PMCF) arrangements, among other key elements.

EUDAMED, established under Article 33 of the MDR, is the European database for medical devices. Its modules have been developed and put into voluntary or mandatory use in phases since 2020. Before EUDAMED mandatory use, manufacturers often submitted SSCPs through other channels or only satisfied documentary requirements. With the relevant EUDAMED modules entering mandatory use, the electronic submission of SSCPs, online validation by notified bodies, version status management, and public access have become statutory compliance actions rather than mere document management issues. The revised MDCG position paper was issued against this post-mandatory-use background to address practical pain points such as unclear statuses, confusing version control, ambiguous access rights, missing language versions, disconnection from notified body validation workflows, and insufficient linkage with post-market surveillance data.

The regulatory significance of this revision therefore lies not only in interpreting existing provisions but also in translating MDR Article 32, Article 33, and Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2021/2078 on EUDAMED into operational rules at the system level. If stakeholders fail to adjust their internal SSCP processes in a timely manner, they may face rejection of validation by notified bodies, outdated public information in EUDAMED, nonconformities identified by competent authorities, and in extreme cases, impact on market access or continued placing on the market of devices.

## Core Changes Analysis

The key changes in this revised MDCG position paper can be summarised in the following seven areas.

### 1. Redefinition of SSCP status model and user roles in EUDAMED

The revision clarifies the status flow of an SSCP in EUDAMED, typically including "draft", "ready for validation", "validation in progress", "validated", "published", "update required", and "withdrawn". As the party responsible for submission, the manufacturer may create drafts, initiate validation requests, and resubmit after rejection. Notified bodies have the authority to validate, reject, request additional information, and approve publication. The revised paper emphasises that once an SSCP has been validated and published by a notified body, the manufacturer cannot directly modify the published record. Any substantive revision must enter the validation process as a new version. This arrangement ensures complete audit trails and version control of SSCPs in EUDAMED, avoiding silent modifications of public documents.

In addition, the position paper reiterates the boundaries of access rights for different EUDAMED participants. Manufacturers must register through the EUDAMED Actor module, and the relevant Local Actor Administrator (LAA) or equivalent administrator should assign SSCP-related permissions. Access rights for notified bodies, competent authorities, and the public are also distinguished to prevent unauthorised actions on validation statuses or unpublished content.

### 2. Update of SSCP drafting template and data structure

The revision further aligns with the standard SSCP templates previously issued by the MDCG (such as the MDCG 2019-9 series) and sets more detailed data structure requirements for the EUDAMED system. Manufacturers are required to use structured fields for device identification, including but not limited to Basic UDI-DI, UDI-DI, trade name, risk class, notified body certificate number, intended purpose, indications, contraindications, target patient population, use environment, and required qualifications for use. The position paper emphasises that the Basic UDI-DI and EUDAMED UDI/device module registration data should be consistent; otherwise, validation may fail.

Moreover, the template update means that SSCPs prepared under older templates may not fully meet the new system field mapping requirements. Manufacturers should conduct a structural gap analysis of existing SSCPs before the next submission or update to ensure that content can be correctly mapped to EUDAMED data fields, rather than being uploaded merely as a PDF attachment.

### 3. Lifecycle management triggers and annual update

Article 32(6) of the MDR requires that SSCPs for Class III and implantable devices be updated at least annually. This position paper further clarifies that the starting point for the annual update should be the date of first validation by the notified body, rather than the device's first placing on the market or the CE certificate issue date. The paper also details triggers in addition to the annual update, including: significant changes to the device, changes in the risk-benefit conclusion due to updated clinical evaluation, new safety or clinical performance conclusions from post-market surveillance (PMS) or PMCF evaluation/report, field safety corrective actions (FSCA), changes in the scope of the notified body certificate, and significant changes in the device registration data in EUDAMED.

For these trigger events, manufacturers should not wait for the annual due date; they should initiate the SSCP revision promptly after the triggering event is completed. The revised paper particularly emphasises that SSCP updates must be based on the latest approved clinical evaluation and post-market surveillance data, and the updated content must be consistent with PSURs, PMCF evaluation reports, and other related documents. When validating an updated version, the notified body will check consistency with those documents.

### 4. Notified body validation process and rejection/remediation

The revision describes the notified body validation responsibilities in process terms. Based on MDR Article 32 and applicable designation documents, notified bodies should verify the completeness of the SSCP content, its consistency with the clinical evaluation report and technical documentation, and whether it contains misleading statements regarding device safety or clinical performance. The position paper does not create a new statutory validation deadline, but it explicitly requires notified bodies to establish standard operating procedures (SOPs) to avoid unnecessary delays caused by opaque processes. At the same time, when rejecting an SSCP, the notified body should provide clear rejection reason codes or explanations in EUDAMED, distinguishing between formal issues, content deficiencies, and inconsistency with technical documentation.

After receiving a rejection, the manufacturer should remedy the issue based on the notified body's comments and resubmit as a new version. If only typographical errors or non-substantive field corrections are involved, some cases may be handled with additional explanation according to the notified body's procedure, but version management rules should not be bypassed to modify published content directly. The position paper also reminds that the notified body's validation does not replace the manufacturer's primary responsibility for the truthfulness and completeness of SSCP content.

### 5. Language version management and non-editable public text

According to Article 32(5) of the MDR, the SSCP should be written in the official languages accepted by the Member States where the device is intended to be made available. This position paper further clarifies that multiple language versions should be uploaded as linked documents under the same SSCP record, rather than creating multiple independent SSCP records in EUDAMED. The base validated version is usually the source language version reviewed by the notified body. Translations must be consistent with the source language version, but in principle the notified body's validation focuses on the source language content, unless relevant regulations or agreements provide otherwise. Manufacturers should ensure that all language versions are internally reviewed before publication to avoid inconsistencies caused by translation errors.

Another important change is that published SSCP texts in EUDAMED cannot be directly edited. Any correction, revision, or replacement of any language version must be incorporated into overall version control, with historical versions retained for audit trail purposes. This places higher demands on manufacturers' translation management, version synchronisation, and document change control.

### 6. Data linkage between SSCP and other EUDAMED modules

EUDAMED is not an isolated database. Its Actor, UDI/device, notified body and certificate, clinical investigation/performance study, vigilance, and market surveillance modules are inherently connected. This position paper emphasises that device identification information, notified body certificate information, UDI-DI/Basic UDI-DI, and other data in the SSCP should be referenced or verified automatically or semi-automatically from relevant EUDAMED modules. Manufacturers should assess whether SSCP updates are needed when updating device registration information, certificate information, or when UDI changes occur. Conversely, if the certificate or UDI data referenced in the SSCP become invalid, EUDAMED may issue a status warning, affecting the public validity of the SSCP.

This linkage mechanism means that SSCP compliance is no longer an isolated document task; it should be integrated into the manufacturer's overall EUDAMED data governance system. Manufacturers should establish cross-functional data consistency review processes to ensure that registration, certificate, PMS, and SSCP module data do not conflict.

### 7. Migration and scope of SSCP for legacy devices during the transitional period

For legacy devices that continue to be placed on the market under the transitional arrangements of Article 120(3) of the MDR, there was no SSCP requirement under the former directive framework. This position paper states that legacy devices only need to prepare and upload an SSCP under MDR Article 32 after obtaining an MDR notified body certificate. For devices that already have MDR certificates and had prepared SSCPs before but had not submitted them to EUDAMED, manufacturers should complete the migration and upload in accordance with the new process after EUDAMED mandatory use begins. The position paper also reminds that clinical safety summaries contained in old directive compliance documents for legacy devices must not be confused with MDR SSCPs.

This provision draws a clear boundary for manufacturers in transition and avoids the omission of SSCP obligations due to misunderstanding of the concept of "legacy devices".

## Impact Analysis

### For manufacturers

For manufacturers of implantable and Class III devices, the biggest impact of this revision is the systematic upgrading of processes. Manufacturers must not only maintain the quality of SSCP content, but also manage electronic statuses, versions, permissions, and language linkages in EUDAMED. Internal quality system documents, such as SOPs and work instructions, need to be updated accordingly, transforming SSCP lifecycle management from paper-based or decentralised document management to an EUDAMED-driven electronic process. For manufacturers with multiple products, multiple languages, and multiple notified bodies, the challenges of change control and data consistency will increase significantly. Failure to operate under the new process may lead to validation delays by notified bodies, outdated public SSCP versions, or nonconformities identified during regulatory audits.

### For notified bodies

Notified bodies' validation work will expand from document review to online operations in EUDAMED, status management, and data consistency checks. The revision requires notified bodies to establish more transparent rejection reasons and validation records, which helps reduce inconsistencies in validation standards but also increases their internal process burden. Notified bodies need to train dedicated staff, become familiar with the SSCP module operations in EUDAMED, and communicate fully with manufacturers before validation. If notified body validation criteria are too strict or inconsistently interpreted, industry compliance costs may further rise.

### For patients and users

From the perspective of patients and healthcare professionals, the public accessibility and standardised version management of SSCPs after EUDAMED mandatory use is a positive development. Patients can find the latest validated safety and clinical performance information in the official EU database, reducing risks caused by information asymmetry. If multilingual linkage management is effectively implemented, it will also improve information accessibility for patients in non-English-speaking countries. However, if language versions are updated with delay or if the content remains too technical, the actual readability and patient-friendliness of SSCPs may remain limited. Industry should continue to focus on the readability and public communication functions of SSCPs, rather than only meeting regulatory field requirements.

## Compliance Recommendations

To address this revised MDCG position paper, stakeholders should take the following concrete actions:

1. **Conduct a gap analysis of current SSCP status.** Manufacturers should compare existing SSCP drafting templates, status management, version records, language versions, and EUDAMED submission processes against the new position paper item by item, identify gaps, form a corrective action list, and close each item.

2. **Update quality system documentation.** Upgrade SSCP management SOPs and work instructions to cover the full lifecycle, including EUDAMED system operations, role permissions, status transitions, version control, annual update triggers, and language version linkage. Clearly define responsibilities across R&D, clinical, regulatory, quality, translation, and EUDAMED administrator functions.

3. **Complete EUDAMED role and permission configuration.** Confirm that Actor module registration information is valid and that the LAA appropriately assigns SSCP creation, submission, viewing, and approval permissions. Avoid permissions errors that could prevent notified body access or manufacturer resubmission.

4. **Adopt the latest SSCP template and prepare structured content.** When preparing or updating an SSCP, use the latest template and ensure that Basic UDI-DI, UDI-DI, certificate number, EMDN/device classification, and other key fields are consistent with other EUDAMED modules. Conduct internal field mapping tests before formal submission.

5. **Establish upfront communication with notified bodies.** Before submission, confirm the notified body's SSCP validation criteria, rejection reason codes, format requirements, and expected timelines. For cases involving multiple notified bodies or multiple certificates, clarify operational differences among notified bodies and specify them in the quality system.

6. **Establish an SSCP annual update calendar and trigger event monitoring.** Link the SSCP annual update date with PSUR cycles, PMCF evaluation reports, CER updates, significant changes, and FSCAs. Set automated reminders to ensure that SSCP revisions are initiated within a reasonable time after trigger events.

7. **Strengthen multilingual version management.** Establish a translation control process to ensure that after the source language version is validated by the notified body, translated versions undergo independent review and remain consistent with the source. Avoid translation errors or version misalignment that could affect the accuracy of public SSCPs and regulatory compliance.

8. **Retain complete electronic submission and validation records.** Although EUDAMED itself preserves an audit trail, manufacturers should internally retain records of submission times, notified body validation comments, rejection reasons, corrective actions, and final published versions, to be available for competent authority review.

9. **Conduct periodic EUDAMED data consistency checks.** When device registration information, UDI, notified body certificate, PMS, or vigilance information changes, initiate an SSCP consistency check to ensure that SSCP data in EUDAMED do not conflict with other modules.

10. **Conduct internal training and simulations.** Train personnel involved in SSCP preparation, review, translation, and EUDAMED operations on the new process, and simulate submission, rejection, and resubmission to reduce error rates in actual operations.

## Timeline and Key Dates

The revised MDCG position paper was published on 30 October 2026. As an EU-level guidance document, the MDCG position paper does not itself set a statutory transition period, but it reflects the unified regulatory expectation on how MDR Article 32, Article 33, and the EUDAMED implementing regulation should be implemented after mandatory use. Stakeholders should initiate process adjustments as soon as possible, and preferably complete gap analysis and quality system updates before the next SSCP submission or update cycle.

For implantable and Class III devices that already have MDR certificates and existing SSCPs, the new process should be fully applied at the first annual update or trigger-event update after EUDAMED mandatory use. For manufacturers that have not yet obtained MDR certificates but are applying or maintaining transitional qualification, they should plan in advance for EUDAMED Actor registration, template preparation, and notified body validation processes to ensure timely SSCP uploads after certificates are issued. Legacy device holders should carefully verify whether their devices have switched to MDR certificates to avoid omitting SSCP obligations.

It is recommended that corporate regulatory affairs teams continue to monitor follow-up MDCG Q&A documents, EUDAMED system release notes, and enforcement practices by national competent authorities, because further details regarding SSCP operations may still be clarified through subsequent documents. In the era of mandatory EUDAMED use, the SSCP has shifted from a static document to a dynamic electronic record. Only by integrating SSCP into the overall lifecycle data governance system can enterprises robustly respond to regulatory changes and safeguard patient safety.
