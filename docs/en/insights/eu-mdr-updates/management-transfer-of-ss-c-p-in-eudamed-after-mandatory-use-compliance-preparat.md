# Management Transfer of SS(C)P in EUDAMED After Mandatory Use: Compliance Preparation and Three Critical Dates for Manufacturers in the Direct Upload Era

> **Document Basis**: MDCG 2026-4 "Management of SS(C)P in EUDAMED After Mandatory Use"
> **Publication Date**: 2 June 2026
> **Applicable Framework**: EU MDR 2017/745 and EU IVDR 2017/746

## ## 1. Policy Background & Significance

### 1.1 What is SS(C)P

SS(C)P is the combined abbreviation for the **Summary of Safety and Clinical Performance (SSCP)** and the **Summary of Safety and Performance (SSP)**, two core public transparency mechanisms established under the EU Medical Device Regulation (MDR) and the In Vitro Diagnostic Medical Devices Regulation (IVDR).

Specifically:

- **SSCP**, pursuant to MDR Article 32, applies to **implantable medical devices** and **all Class III devices** (except where exempted). The manufacturer must prepare the SSCP, which is then reviewed and validated by a Notified Body (NB) before being made publicly available through EUDAMED.
- **SSP**, pursuant to IVDR Article 29, applies to **Class C and Class D in vitro diagnostic medical devices**, with essentially the same requirements and public disclosure pathway as the SSCP.

The content of the SSCP/SSP must cover the intended purpose of the device, key performance and safety data, risk-benefit analysis conclusions, a summary of clinical/performance evaluation data, post-market surveillance information, and any residual risks and uncertainties. The target readership includes patients and healthcare professionals, with the goal of achieving "transparency of key safety information accessible to the public" for higher-risk device classes.

### 1.2 Regulatory Background of the Responsibility Transfer

The core change introduced by MDCG 2026-4 is that **after EUDAMED enters mandatory use, the responsibility for uploading SS(C)P transfers from the Notified Body to the manufacturer.**

The underlying logic of this change can be understood from several perspectives:

**First, the functional evolution of EUDAMED.** EUDAMED (the European Database on Medical Devices) has been positioned since the drafting of the 2017 Regulations as the central information platform for medical device supervision in the EU. Under MDR Article 33, EUDAMED comprises six interconnected electronic systems, with the "actor registration system" serving as its primary data entry point. However, EUDAMED's development cycle far exceeded initial expectations. During the transition period, the EU adopted a "phased availability" strategy, allowing modules to be used voluntarily once validated. Once EUDAMED reached mandatory use status, all statutory information disclosure obligations must be fulfilled through the platform.

**Second, the logical correction of responsibility allocation.** In the original text of MDR Article 32 and IVDR Article 29, the Regulations only require that "the manufacturer prepares, the Notified Body validates, and EUDAMED makes it public" — without explicitly assigning who performs the upload. Early MDCG guidance (e.g., MDCG 2019-9 on SSCP and MDCG 2021-1 Rev.1 on SSP) assigned the upload responsibility to Notified Bodies in the context where EUDAMED was not yet fully available, to ensure timely public disclosure of SSCP/SSP documents. However, having the NB continuously manage upload responsibilities after validation both increased the administrative burden on NBs and placed them in an unreasonable role in the full-lifecycle document update management — because the annual update obligation for post-market SSCP/SSP clearly rests with the manufacturer (MDR Article 32(6); IVDR Article 29(6)).

**Third, the return of Notified Bodies to their core function.** The statutory core function of a Notified Body is conformity assessment, not the ongoing management of post-market information disclosure. Returning the upload responsibility to manufacturers allows NBs to focus on their validation and oversight roles, avoiding systemic responsibilities they should not bear at the EUDAMED operational level, while also reducing the information transfer chain between manufacturers and NBs and mitigating version inconsistency risks.

### 1.3 Policy Positioning and Strategic Significance

The issuance of MDCG 2026-4 marks the institutional transition point of EUDAMED from "voluntary use during the transition period" to "mandatory use." This guidance is not merely a technical operational instruction; it represents an institutional calibration within the EU's MDR/IVDR implementation: **whoever bears the statutory update obligation also bears the platform disclosure obligation.**

For manufacturers, this shift sends an important compliance signal: EUDAMED is no longer merely a "database operated on behalf of the manufacturer by the Notified Body," but rather a statutory infrastructure that manufacturers must deeply embed into their quality management systems and post-market surveillance systems.

## ## 2. Core Changes Analysis

### 2.1 First Core Change: Transfer of Upload Responsibility from Notified Body to Manufacturer

MDCG 2026-4 explicitly provides that after mandatory EUDAMED use, the obligation to upload SS(C)P shifts from the Notified Body to the **manufacturer**. This means:

- The manufacturer must personally complete the upload of the master SS(C)P document in EUDAMED;
- The manufacturer must personally upload SS(C)P translations;
- The manufacturer must ensure that the uploaded version is **completely identical** to the version validated by the Notified Body during certification;
- The manufacturer must ensure that the uploaded version remains synchronized with the currently valid SS(C)P version.

The legal basis for this change lies in MDR Article 32(3) and IVDR Article 29(3), which provide that the manufacturer must submit the validated SS(C)P, together with the validation date, to EUDAMED for public disclosure. Once EUDAMED mandatory use takes effect, the executor of the "submission" naturally reverts to the manufacturer.

After validating the SS(C)P, the Notified Body no longer performs the EUDAMED upload; however, the NB's **validation responsibility remains unchanged** — manufacturers must still submit the SS(C)P draft to the NB for review and approval before uploading.

### 2.2 Second Core Change: Three-Phase Transition Timeline

MDCG 2026-4 establishes a clear phased transition arrangement to ensure no gap in information disclosure during the handover of responsibilities:

#### Phase 1: Transition Period (28 May 2026 – 30 September 2026)

Starting from 28 May 2026 (the start date of EUDAMED mandatory use), a transition period commences. During this period:

- **Notified Bodies continue to upload** their validated SS(C)P documents;
- Manufacturers should complete their **Actor Registration** and **user access configuration** in EUDAMED during this period;
- Manufacturers should carry out internal preparations, including updates to quality system documents, SOP development, personnel training, and data review.

This transition period is designed to provide manufacturers with approximately four months of preparation time to ensure they are capable of independently assuming upload responsibilities in the next phase.

#### Phase 2: Direct Upload by Manufacturers (from October 2026)

From October 2026, manufacturers must **directly upload** SS(C)P documents to EUDAMED. Requirements in this phase include:

- All newly validated SS(C)P documents are uploaded by the manufacturer;
- The manufacturer must upload the **master SS(C)P** and all applicable translations;
- The manufacturer must ensure the uploaded document matches the NB-validated version and retain validation evidence.

#### Phase 3: Deadline for Legacy Device Retroactive Uploads (by 27 February 2027)

For **already-marketed devices** (i.e., devices that have obtained CE certification and whose SS(C)P has been validated by the NB but not yet uploaded by the manufacturer), the manufacturer must complete the EUDAMED SS(C)P upload by **27 February 2027**. This deadline provides approximately four months for retroactive uploads, calculated from the October 2026 start of direct manufacturer uploads.

### 2.3 Third Core Change: Version Consistency Requirement

MDCG 2026-4 particularly emphasizes that the SS(C)P version uploaded by the manufacturer must be **identical to the version validated by the Notified Body at the time of certification**. This means:

- The manufacturer must not make any content modification to the NB-validated SS(C)P without re-validation;
- Translations, while not requiring word-by-word NB validation, must faithfully reflect the validated master content and must not alter the technical meaning;
- If the SS(C)P content changes after validation (e.g., due to annual update obligations, new clinical data, changes in risk-benefit conclusions), the manufacturer must **first submit the updated version to the Notified Body for validation** before uploading the updated version to EUDAMED;
- The manufacturer should establish a version archive recording the validation date, the validating NB, and the upload date for each SS(C)P version, to demonstrate the integrity of the compliance chain.

### 2.4 Fourth Core Change: Master Document and Translation Management

MDCG 2026-4 explicitly states that manufacturers must upload the "master SS(C)P and translations":

- **Master SS(C)P**: The master version is the language version used by the Notified Body for validation, typically the NB's working language (most often English, but possibly another language depending on the NB-manufacturer agreement);
- **Translations**: Manufacturers must provide SS(C)P translations in the official languages of their device's target markets. Translations must accurately convey the master content, particularly the accuracy and completeness of safety-related information;
- **Upload scope**: Manufacturers should upload all produced and appropriately reviewed translation versions. While the MDR does not explicitly mandate coverage of all Member State official languages, manufacturers should cover target market language needs as comprehensively as possible from the perspective of patient accessibility and regulatory compliance;
- **Translation review**: Translations do not require NB validation, but manufacturers should establish translation control procedures within their quality management system (e.g., review by bilingual personnel with medical or regulatory backgrounds or professional translation vendors) and retain translation review records.

### 2.5 Fifth Core Change: EUDAMED Operational Requirements

MDCG 2026-4 also sets specific requirements for manufacturers' EUDAMED operational capabilities:

- **Actor Registration**: Manufacturers must complete Actor registration in EUDAMED, obtain a Single Registration Number (SRN), and ensure that the device data associated with the SS(C)P upload (UDI-DI, Basic UDI-DI, etc.) is accurate;
- **User Access Control**: Manufacturers must designate authorized EUDAMED users and assign appropriate upload and document management permissions;
- **Data Consistency**: Device registration data in EUDAMED must be consistent with the device information described in the SS(C)P, the certificate information, and the data in the manufacturer's technical documentation;
- **Update Maintenance**: Manufacturers must integrate EUDAMED SS(C)P maintenance into their post-market surveillance (PMS) and post-market clinical follow-up (PMCF) processes to ensure continuous information updates.

## ## 3. Impact Analysis

### 3.1 Impact on Manufacturers

#### Increased Compliance Obligations

Manufacturers face a new **direct statutory operational obligation** — the upload and management of SS(C)P in EUDAMED. This is not simply "changing the operator"; it is a significant change that embeds EUDAMED into the manufacturer's daily compliance operations:

- Manufacturers must establish or strengthen EUDAMED operational capabilities, including technical infrastructure (such as network environment and security certificates supporting EUDAMED access) and human resources (trained EUDAMED operators);
- The manufacturer's Quality Management System (QMS) must add procedural documents and work instructions for SS(C)P upload management, version control, translation management, and EUDAMED data maintenance;
- Manufacturers must establish a full-process tracking mechanism from SS(C)P preparation → NB validation → EUDAMED upload → annual update.

#### Time Pressure

The October 2026 start of direct uploads means manufacturers only have approximately four months of transition time to prepare. For manufacturers with extensive product portfolios, multi-country translation requirements, or a large number of SS(C)P documents, this period is extremely tight. In particular, for the 27 February 2027 legacy device upload deadline, manufacturers must complete the review and upload of all already-marketed device SS(C)P documents within approximately four months.

#### Version Consistency Risk

The core risk in the version consistency requirement lies here: if the manufacturer fails to properly archive the NB-validated final version, or if internal document management is chaotic, it is highly likely that a compliance incident of "uploading the wrong version" will occur during EUDAMED upload — the uploaded SS(C)P does not match the NB-validated version, which in regulatory inspections may be deemed a failure to comply with MDR Article 32(3), triggering corrective actions or even market surveillance measures.

#### Translation Management Burden

For manufacturers marketing implantable/Class III devices or Class C/D IVDs in multiple countries, the production, review, upload, and continuous updating of translations will constitute an ongoing operational cost and compliance burden. By explicitly including translations in the manufacturer's upload obligations, MDCG 2026-4 makes translation management a statutory obligation rather than a "nice-to-have" practice.

### 3.2 Impact on the Industry

#### Recalibration of Notified Body Roles

Once relieved of EUDAMED upload responsibilities, Notified Bodies' audit resources can be redirected towards their core conformity assessment functions — including SS(C)P content validation, periodic audits, and post-market surveillance reviews. This institutionally reduces the administrative burden on NBs, contributing to shorter validation cycles and higher review quality.

However, short-term transition costs must be considered: during the transition period (28 May – 30 September 2026), NBs continue uploading, and manufacturers and NBs must reach clear arrangements for the handover of legacy devices to avoid omissions or duplicate uploads due to communication gaps.

#### Changes in the Regulatory Services Market

Professional service providers specializing in regulatory affairs and EUDAMED operations may see new market demand — particularly for small and medium-sized manufacturers, establishing EUDAMED operational teams, producing multilingual translations, and managing version consistency can all be met through outsourcing or consulting services. This will generate short-term compliance service demand.

#### Industry Standardization Trends

As upload responsibility transfers to manufacturers, standardized practices for full-lifecycle SS(C)P management will gradually emerge, including SSCP document structure specifications (per the MDCG 2019-9 templates), translation review checklists, version control SOPs, and more. Industry associations may leverage this opportunity to promote unified best practices, reducing compliance level discrepancies among manufacturers.

### 3.3 Impact on Patients and Users

From the perspective of patients and users, this change should **not fundamentally produce a negative user experience impact** — EUDAMED's function and access methods as a public database remain unchanged. However, potential positive impacts include:

- **Improved information timeliness**: Direct manufacturer upload eliminates the NB intermediary step, allowing SS(C)P updates (especially annual updates) to appear in EUDAMED more promptly, enabling patients and healthcare professionals to access the latest safety and performance information in a more timely manner;
- **Ensured information integrity**: The version consistency requirement ensures that the SS(C)P publicly disclosed in EUDAMED is completely identical to the content strictly validated by the NB, avoiding version deviations that previously could arise from multi-step transmission;
- **Expanded translation coverage**: MDCG 2026-4 encourages comprehensive upload of translations, potentially enabling more patients to read the SS(C)P in their own language and strengthening the informed basis for medical decision-making.

It should be noted that if manufacturers fail to effectively manage version consistency or translation quality, patients may encounter inaccurate or incomplete information in the SS(C)P — an area that regulators must focus on in subsequent market surveillance.

## ## 4. Compliance Recommendations

### 4.1 Immediately Confirm EUDAMED Readiness (June–August 2026)

Manufacturers should immediately undertake the following:

1. **Confirm/complete Actor registration**: Verify the company's actor identity in EUDAMED, ensure the SRN is valid and role information is accurate;
2. **Configure user permissions**: Confirm that authorized employees possess EUDAMED upload permissions and establish a two-person review mechanism (where applicable);
3. **Test upload procedures**: Conduct EUDAMED test environment or rehearsal uploads early in the transition period to familiarize with system operations and data field requirements;
4. **Audit device data**: Verify that UDI-DI, Basic UDI-DI, and certificate information registered in EUDAMED are consistent with the SSCP descriptions and technical documentation.

### 4.2 Establish SS(C)P Version Control and Document Management System

1. **Compile an SS(C)P inventory**: Establish a complete list of all devices requiring public SS(C)P (implantable devices, Class III devices, Class C/D IVDs), recording the version number, validation date, validating NB, and change history for each SS(C)P;
2. **Lock validated versions**: Confirm with the Notified Body the official validated version documents (including validation dates) for each SS(C)P, ensuring that these version documents are fully retained and designated as "master versions";
3. **Establish a translation archive**: Develop a translation plan, clarify language needs for each target market, and establish a mapping archive between translation versions and master versions;
4. **Develop version update procedures**: Clarify in the QMS the trigger conditions for SS(C)P updates (annual updates, new clinical data, design changes, risk-benefit changes, etc.), the NB re-validation process, and the interface requirements for EUDAMED uploads.

### 4.3 Adjust Cooperation Arrangements with Notified Bodies

1. **Confirm handover responsibilities**: Confirm in writing with the NB the upload responsibility allocation during the transition period (28 May – 30 September 2026), clearly identifying which SS(C)P documents will be uploaded by the NB during the transition and which will be uploaded by the manufacturer from October 2026 onwards;
2. **Obtain validated document lists**: Obtain from the NB the final versions and validation records of all validated SS(C)P documents, ensuring that manufacturers possess complete archives;
3. **Establish future validation pathways**: Confirm with the NB the submission and validation procedures for updated SS(C)P versions, ensuring that all future updates are validated before upload.

### 4.4 Develop a Backward-Planned Schedule for the Deadline

For the **27 February 2027** legacy device upload deadline, manufacturers should develop a detailed backward-planned schedule:

| Timeline | Action Required |
|----------|----------------|
| June–July 2026 | Complete SS(C)P inventory and version verification |
| August 2026 | Complete EUDAMED Actor registration/permission configuration, personnel training |
| September 2026 | Complete upload process rehearsals and document preparation |
| October 2026 | Begin direct upload of newly validated SS(C)P documents |
| October–December 2026 | Prioritize legacy SS(C)P uploads for high-risk, high-volume products |
| January–February 2027 | Complete all legacy SS(C)P uploads and conduct final verification |

### 4.5 Update Quality Management System Documents

Manufacturers must add or revise the following QMS documents:

- **SOP: SS(C)P Preparation and Validation Process** (including NB submission and validation tracking);
- **SOP: EUDAMED SS(C)P Upload and Version Management** (including upload verification and error correction procedures);
- **SOP: SS(C)P Translation Management and Quality Control**;
- **Record Forms**: SS(C)P status tracking form (version number, validation date, upload date, translation language list, update history).

### 4.6 Personnel Training

The transfer of upload responsibility is not purely an IT operational matter but a cross-functional task involving regulatory affairs, clinical, quality, and IT. Recommended training includes:

- **Regulatory Affairs team**: Full interpretation of MDCG 2026-4, regulatory consequences of version inconsistency;
- **Clinical/Quality teams**: Identification of trigger conditions for SS(C)P content maintenance and update obligations;
- **IT/Operations teams**: EUDAMED system operation, permission management, common error handling;
- **Management**: Resource investment needs, consequences of non-compliance (including market surveillance risks).

### 4.7 Special Recommendations for SMEs

For small and medium-sized manufacturers with limited resources:

- **Prioritize completing the minimum necessary work**: Confirm EUDAMED access rights and ensure at least login and operational capability;
- **Consider outsourcing translations and EUDAMED management**: Outsource non-core operations to professional service providers, but establish supplier management procedures within the QMS;
- **Maintain close communication with NBs**: Leverage the transition period when NBs are still undertaking some upload tasks to rapidly build internal capability;
- **Utilize industry resources**: Participate in training and webinars organized by MDCG and industry organizations to obtain templates, checklists, and other tools.

## ## 5. Timeline & Key Dates

The key milestones established by MDCG 2026-4 are as follows:

| Date | Milestone | Description |
|------|-----------|-------------|
| 28 May 2026 | EUDAMED mandatory use commences | EUDAMED officially enters mandatory use phase; transition period begins |
| 28 May 2026 – 30 September 2026 | Transition period | Notified Bodies continue uploading validated SS(C)P; manufacturers should complete EUDAMED preparations during this period |
| 1 October 2026 | Direct manufacturer uploads commence | Newly validated SS(C)P documents are uploaded directly by manufacturers to EUDAMED |
| 27 February 2027 | Legacy device upload deadline | All already-marketed device SS(C)P documents must be uploaded to EUDAMED by manufacturers |

## ## Conclusion

The issuance of MDCG 2026-4 appears on the surface to be a technical transfer of SS(C)P upload operational responsibilities within the EUDAMED system. In substance, it represents an institutional evolution of the EU medical device regulatory system from "Notified Body agency management" to "full manufacturer responsibility." Manufacturers should fully recognize the profound significance of this change: **information disclosure is no longer an ancillary step following certification, but a continuous compliance obligation spanning the entire device lifecycle.**

For Chinese medical device manufacturers, if their products involve implantable devices, Class III devices, or Class C/D IVDs marketed in the EU, MDCG 2026-4 has direct applicability. It is recommended that affected enterprises immediately initiate internal preparations upon publication of this guidance, ensure EUDAMED SS(C)P direct upload capability before October 2026, and complete all legacy device compliance uploads by 27 February 2027, to avoid market surveillance risks arising from failure to fulfill upload obligations.
