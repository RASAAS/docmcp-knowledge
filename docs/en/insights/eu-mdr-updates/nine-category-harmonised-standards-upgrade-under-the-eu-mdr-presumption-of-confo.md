## 1. Policy Background and Significance

On 17 June 2026, the European Commission published Implementing Decision (EU) 2026/1231 in the Official Journal of the European Union, amending Implementing Decision (EU) 2021/1182 and updating the list of harmonised standards under Regulation (EU) 2017/745 on medical devices (MDR). The legal effective date of the Decision is 11 June 2026. The update covers nine major categories of harmonised standards for medical devices, representing another concentrated "standard version revision" since the MDR became applicable, directly changing the technical basis on which relevant devices obtain a "presumption of conformity".

Article 8 of the MDR provides that devices which are in conformity with harmonised standards, or the relevant parts thereof, published in the Official Journal of the European Union shall be presumed to be in conformity with the requirements of the MDR covered by those standards or parts thereof, in particular the general safety and performance requirements (GSPRs). This presumption mechanism makes harmonised standards the common technical language for manufacturers choosing conformity paths, notified bodies carrying out conformity assessment, and competent authorities conducting market surveillance. Therefore, once the version of a harmonised standard is updated, the old version may no longer provide a presumption of conformity after the date of cessation specified in the Annex. Manufacturers can no longer automatically rely on old test reports and documentation.

Implementing Decision (EU) 2021/1182 initially listed a set of harmonised standards under the MDR and has been amended several times. Implementing Decision (EU) 2026/1231 further incorporates the latest versions or amendments, including EN ISO 10993-12:2021/A1:2025 and EN ISO 15223-1:2021/A1:2025, into the MDR framework. This reflects the absorption by European and international standardisation organisations of technical progress in areas such as biological compatibility, labelling symbols, electrical safety, and blood transfusion equipment safety. For manufacturers, this is both an update of compliance obligations and a critical window to systematically review product technical documentation and declarations of conformity.

## 2. Core Changes Analysis

The core change introduced by this Decision is the amendment of the version information for harmonised standards in the Annex to Implementing Decision (EU) 2021/1182. Manufacturers should consult the Annex to the Decision and the specific entries in the Official Journal, taking the standard number, version or amendment, and the "date of cessation of presumption of conformity" as authoritative. The following is an analysis by nine categories.

### 1. Biological evaluation — EN ISO 10993-12:2021/A1:2025

EN ISO 10993-12:2021/A1:2025 is formally incorporated into the harmonised standards under the MDR. This standard is the core sample preparation and reference material standard for biological evaluation of medical devices, linking ISO 10993-17 toxicological risk assessment and ISO 10993-18 chemical characterisation. Amendment A1:2025 revises key steps such as sample preparation, extraction conditions and reference materials, which may affect the comparability of data from leachables/extractables studies, identification of unknown substances, and toxicological risk assessment. If manufacturers continue to refer to the old EN ISO 10993-12:2021, they are likely to be considered as not meeting the latest state of the art during technical documentation review by notified bodies.

### 2. Labelling symbols — EN ISO 15223-1:2021/A1:2025

EN ISO 15223-1:2021/A1:2025 updates the labelling symbol requirements for medical devices, directly linked to Chapter 23 of Annex I to the MDR on labels and instructions for use. The amendment may add or revise symbols for single use, single patient, drug-containing devices, health applications, and strengthen requirements for symbol size, legibility, format consistency, and equivalent use of electronic labelling. Labelling is a frequent inspection point in market surveillance. Version discrepancies may result in labels failing to meet GSPR 23 and trigger non-conformity notifications.

### 3. Electrical medical equipment

The electrical medical equipment category involves the latest amendments to the EN 60601 series of standards on basic safety and essential performance, electromagnetic compatibility, usability, and related aspects. Under the MDR, electrical equipment is considered higher risk. Updates to harmonised standards generally reflect stricter requirements for insulation coordination, software safety, alarm systems, usability engineering, and electromagnetic compatibility immunity. Manufacturers need to reassess their risk management files, safety testing, and software lifecycle documentation.

### 4. Blood transfusion equipment

Updates to blood transfusion equipment standards focus on the safety of blood-contact materials, pyrogen-free status, sterility assurance, biological compatibility, haemolysis, compatibility, and labelling and traceability requirements. If leachables or compatibility problems occur in transfusion devices, patients may be directly endangered. Therefore, the citation of new standards will require supplementary material characterisation, stability studies, and preclinical performance data.

### 5. Ophthalmic optics

Harmonised standards in the ophthalmic optics category may cover intraocular lenses (IOLs), contact lenses, ophthalmic viscoelastic devices, and similar products. The update focuses on optical performance, surface properties, biological compatibility, preclinical evaluation, and labelling information, with particularly higher requirements for long-term optical stability and material durability of implantable products such as foldable intraocular lenses.

### 6. Non-active surgical implants

The non-active surgical implants category covers osteosynthesis, spinal, joint, endovascular implants, and similar devices. New citations usually strengthen static and dynamic mechanical testing, surface modification and coating evaluation, packaging and sterilisation validation, and verification methods for implant-specific risks such as magnetic resonance compatibility. For implant manufacturers, any change in test methods may require new test reports and updated technical documentation.

### 7. Washer-disinfectors

The washer-disinfectors category mainly relates to the EN ISO 15883 series. The update can affect validation of equipment used for reprocessing medical devices. The MDR moves reprocessing validation responsibility forward to the manufacturer, who must demonstrate that the device can be effectively cleaned, disinfected and sterilised according to the instructions. New standards and amendments impose stricter requirements on temperature, cleaning agents, loading, validation protocols, and self-disinfection performance.

### 8. Prosthetics

The prosthetics category covers structural safety, materials, and performance of external prostheses. The update affects strength, fatigue, joint life, skin-contact materials, and user fit evaluation. Manufacturers may need to retest and update instructions for use, especially for load-bearing prostheses and customised components under defined boundary conditions.

### 9. Sharps injury protection

Updates to standards on sharps injury protection, such as those for needle devices with anti-needlestick mechanisms and blood collection needles, strengthen design requirements to prevent needlestick injuries and bloodborne infections among healthcare workers. GSPR 11 of Annex I to the MDR explicitly requires that devices be designed to reduce the risk of infection and injury. The new standards may specify stricter testing of activation, locking, irreversibility, and reliability of protective mechanisms, directly affecting the effectiveness of device safety mechanisms.

## 3. Impact Analysis

### For manufacturers

Manufacturers are the primary duty bearers in this standards update. Under Article 10 of the MDR, manufacturers must establish, document, implement and maintain technical documentation to ensure compliance with the GSPRs. After the harmonised standard versions are updated, manufacturers must at least: identify affected standard references product by product; conduct a gap analysis between the old and new versions; assess whether supplementary testing is needed; update technical documentation, risk management files, clinical evaluation data, labelling and instructions for use; and update the EU declaration of conformity.

For products already CE marked and placed on the market, an update to harmonised standards does not automatically invalidate the existing certificate, but it weakens the presumption of conformity provided by the old version. If the manufacturer continues to rely on old test reports after the standard has passed its date of cessation, regulators or notified bodies may consider that the product is not at the latest state of the art. Manufacturers should be able to demonstrate that the product still complies with the GSPRs, or complete necessary design changes and testing and then update the documentation.

For products not yet CE certified and still under application, notified bodies will review technical documentation against the latest harmonised standards. If manufacturers still cite old versions, notified bodies may raise non-conformities, leading to longer certification timelines. For significant changes requiring notified body involvement, manufacturers should assess the change level under Article 56 of the MDR and notify the notified body with updated technical documentation where necessary.

### For the industry

Notified bodies will simultaneously update their technical documentation review checklists and testing capability requirements. Testing laboratories need to obtain accreditation scope for the new standards or amendments; otherwise manufacturers may not be able to issue valid reports because the laboratory lacks qualification. Upstream suppliers and contract manufacturers must also respond to harmonised standard updates by providing material characterisation, biological data, electrical safety data and other deliverables meeting the new versions. Industry associations may issue best practice guidance, but ultimate responsibility remains with the manufacturer.

### For patients and users

The harmonised standard updates ultimately help improve device safety and performance. Stricter biological compatibility testing, clearer labelling symbols, more reliable electrical safety protection, and better sharps injury protection can reduce the risks of infection, injury or use error for patients. At the same time, in the short term, manufacturers may need to supplement testing or update labelling and may adjust marketing plans, which could cause delays in supply of some products or slower registration progress.

## 4. Compliance Recommendations

### 1. Establish a standards impact assessment mechanism

Manufacturers should immediately obtain the full text of Decision (EU) 2026/1231 and its Annex as published in the Official Journal, establish a product-to-standard mapping table, and identify all affected product families, models, and standard references. Incorporate standard version changes into the change control process and assess impacts on design, testing, production, labelling, and supply chain.

### 2. Conduct a standard gap analysis

Compare the technical differences between the old and new versions item by item, especially sample preparation, test conditions, acceptance criteria, labelling symbols, and risk management documentation requirements. For key differences affecting safety and performance, R&D, regulatory, quality, and clinical teams should jointly evaluate and, where necessary, develop a supplementary test plan.

### 3. Update technical documentation and declaration of conformity

Update standard references, the GSPR conformity matrix, risk analysis, and verification and validation reports in the technical documentation in accordance with Annexes II and III of the MDR. The EU declaration of conformity should update the applied harmonised standard versions in accordance with Annex IV of the MDR. Ensure that the standard versions cited in technical documentation and declarations are consistent with the latest Official Journal publication, and keep change history records.

### 4. Assess change level and communicate with notified body

If use of a new standard leads to changes in product design, materials, processes or intended use, the manufacturer must assess whether the change is significant under Article 56 of the MDR. For changes likely to affect safety performance or technical documentation conclusions, communicate with the notified body in advance and submit a change notification or supplementary assessment information as required. For changes that are not significant, prepare written justification and risk assessment records.

### 5. Strengthen laboratory and supply chain management

Confirm that contracted laboratories have testing capability and accreditation scope for the new standards or amendments. Update technical agreements with suppliers and contract manufacturers to clarify delivery requirements for data, documents, and labelling under the new versions. For biological compatibility, electrical safety, and other tests requiring external laboratories, arrange testing schedules early to avoid project delays caused by limited testing resources.

### 6. Manage labelling and instructions for use switching

For labelling symbol standard updates, manufacturers should inventory current labels and instructions for use, assess whether symbols need to be redesigned, information supplemented, or electronic labelling adjusted. Develop a label switching plan and control old label inventory to avoid continuing to use non-compliant labels after the new standard becomes effective. Provide customers and users with necessary transition information where relevant.

### 7. Internal training and continuous monitoring

Regulatory affairs, R&D, quality, and marketing departments should be trained on this harmonised standards update and its impact on day-to-day work. Establish a periodic monitoring mechanism for harmonised standards published in the Official Journal of the European Union, track subsequent amendments and implementing decisions, and ensure that products remain at the latest state of the art throughout their full life cycle.

## 5. Timeline and Key Dates

Implementing Decision (EU) 2026/1231 was published on 17 June 2026, with an effective date of 11 June 2026. Manufacturers should treat 11 June 2026 as the reference date for the change in regulatory status and use the "date of cessation" listed for each standard entry in the Annex to the Official Journal as the specific deadline after which the old version no longer provides a presumption of conformity.

It is important to note that the effective date of the Decision does not necessarily mean that all old versions immediately cease to be valid. Different standards may have different dates of cessation, and some standards may allow transitional use. Manufacturers must check the standard information in the Annex one by one, develop a switch timetable, and complete the necessary updates to drawings, testing, documentation, and labelling before the old standard ceases to be valid.

For ongoing conformity assessment projects, manufacturers should immediately confirm with their notified body whether the new version applies and whether the current technical documentation submission requires supplementary information. For products already CE certified, this harmonised standards update should be incorporated into post-market surveillance and quality system change management to ensure continued compliance with the MDR.
